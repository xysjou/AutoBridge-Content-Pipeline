## What to evaluate

Start with the intended market, driving conditions, passenger or cargo needs, and the total ownership horizon. Confirm the exact trim, powertrain, safety equipment, charging or fuel requirements, and parts availability. Treat advertised specifications as a starting point: request the build sheet, production date, warranty terms, and an independent inspection before money changes hands.

## Process and risk controls

A dependable workflow has five checkpoints: source verification, technical inspection, landed-cost calculation, documentation review, and post-delivery support. Keep one record for the vehicle identification number, invoices, inspection photographs, shipping documents, customs entries, and handover notes. Build a contingency for port storage, currency movement, taxes, repairs, and delayed parts. Clear acceptance criteria reduce disputes between buyers, agents, and workshops.

## Practical recommendation

Compare at least two compliant options using the same assumptions. Prefer transparent suppliers who can explain exclusions and provide traceable service history. If evidence is missing, pause the transaction and ask for it rather than relying on a verbal promise. The right choice is the one that remains supportable after delivery, not simply the one with the lowest headline price.

## Checklist

Before publishing or approving a case, confirm identity and specification; verify legal import eligibility; calculate duty, freight, insurance, and local fees; inspect battery, structure, tyres, electronics, and safety systems as applicable; record warranty contacts; and schedule a first service review. Revisit the checklist whenever regulations, model years, or supplier terms change.
