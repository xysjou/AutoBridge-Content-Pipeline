## Positioning: Why a DM-i Is Different to Spec

The Qin Plus DM-i is a **plug-in hybrid (PHEV) compact sedan**, not a pure EV and not a conventional petrol car. BYD's DM-i system pairs a small, efficiency-focused 1.5L naturally aspirated engine with an electric motor and E-CVT, tuned to run on electric drive as much as possible and use the engine mainly for efficient generation/high-speed assist. For an importer this means three things must be specified together: **battery size, electric-only range version, and motor output** — quoting "a Qin Plus DM-i" without the 55/120 version is ambiguous. This page covers the **2024 Glory Edition (荣耀版, launched February 2024), REFERENCE MARKET: CHINA**. Overseas, the platform appears under names such as Seal 05 DM-i in selected markets; destination-market official specs govern those cars.

## Reading the Range Figures Correctly

A PHEV carries **two** range numbers, and buyers often conflate them:

- **Electric-only range** is given in both NEDC and WLTC (e.g., 120 km NEDC = 101 km WLTC for the big battery). Always state the cycle; the two standards are not interchangeable.
- **Combined range** (fuel + full battery, Chinese conditions) is a separate, much larger figure and should not be marketed as electric range.

For export markets that use their own test protocol or that tax PHEVs by electric km/CO₂, request the destination-certified values rather than copying the Chinese NEDC label.

## Charging, Fuel and Daily-Use Implications

Because the small-battery 55 version has only 8.32 kWh, its AC charging behaviour and charge time differ from the 18.32 kWh 120 version; Chinese cars use the GB/T connector standard and a Chinese-market infotainment/charging ecosystem. Confirm for your order: onboard AC charging capability, whether DC fast charging is included on the chosen grade, connector standard versus destination infrastructure (see our GB/T vs CCS2/CHAdeMO guide), and the fuel grade the 1.5L engine expects. A PHEV still needs a working fuel supply and service path for its engine, not only charging.

## What Exporters Should Pin Down Before Payment

- State the exact version: **55KM or 120KM**, with matching motor (132/145 kW) and battery (8.32/18.32 kWh) on the VIN build sheet.
- Record electric-range cycle (NEDC vs WLTC) and obtain destination-certified PHEV range/CO₂ where required for tax or incentives.
- Confirm charging connector and AC/DC capability by grade; do not assume GB/T matches destination sockets.
- Confirm steering side, infotainment language/OTA outside China, and warranty terms for a China-built PHEV (engine + battery + electric motor all need coverage).
- Verify the destination country's PHEV classification and import treatment — hybrid incentives and homologation differ from pure EVs.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**What is the difference between the 55 and 120 Qin Plus DM-i?** The 120 version has a larger 18.32 kWh battery, a stronger 145 kW motor, longer electric range and quicker acceleration; the 55 uses an 8.32 kWh battery and 132 kW motor.

**Is the 1.5L engine the main drive motor?** In DM-i it is a small 81 kW efficiency engine used mainly for generation and assist; the electric motor (132 or 145 kW) provides primary drive.

**Why are there two electric-range numbers per version?** China labels both NEDC and WLTC; e.g., the 120 version is 120 km NEDC / 101 km WLTC. Always quote the cycle.

**Does the combined 1245 km mean it drives 1245 km on electricity?** No — that is a combined fuel-plus-battery Chinese figure; pure-electric driving is 120 km (NEDC) at most.

**Is the Qin Plus DM-i sold abroad?** The platform appears in selected markets under names such as Seal 05 DM-i; confirm destination-market naming and official specs rather than using Chinese sheets as global specs.
