## The Misconception That Costs Importers Money

Many first-time buyers assume that under FOB or CIF the **shipping line will fully compensate** a scratched, dented, stolen or crushed vehicle. It will not. A carrier's liability is capped by international convention far below a car's value, and the cargo insurance you choose determines whether ordinary transit damage is covered at all. This guide covers **marine cargo insurance for vehicles shipped from China** (global ocean routes) — it is about insurance clauses and claims, a different topic from Incoterms price checks or RoRo-vs-container choice.

## The Three Cover Tiers: ICC (A), (B), (C)

Institute Cargo Clauses come in three levels (cross-checked across shipping/insurance guides):

| Tier | What it covers (outline) | Suitability for vehicles |
|---|---|---|
| **ICC (A) — "All Risks"** | Accidental physical loss/damage except listed exclusions; **includes theft, scratching and loading/unloading damage** | Recommended for vehicles |
| **ICC (B) — named perils** | Fire/explosion, stranding/sinking, collision, water ingress, etc. (named events only) | Leaves common vehicle losses uncovered |
| **ICC (C) — basic** | Major casualties only (e.g., total-loss-type events) | Inadequate for a car |

The practical reason vehicles warrant **ICC (A)** is precisely the losses B and C exclude: **loading/unloading damage, theft and many scratch/dent scenarios** — the most frequent problems on RoRo and in port handling. For high-value, luxury or classic vehicles, All-Risks is the baseline; **war-risk and strikes-risk are separate add-ons** that must be purchased additionally for sensitive routes.

## Setting the Insured Value (No Fixed Premium Here)

Standard CIF/CIP and letter-of-credit practice sets minimum insured value at **110% of CIF value** (100% goods value + 10% for expected profit/charges), cross-checked across logistics sources:

- **Insured amount = CIF value × 110%**
- **Premium = insured amount × insurance rate**

The **rate floats with route, season, cover tier, vessel and cargo risk and must be quoted per shipment** — no fixed percentage is published here, because a printed rate would be wrong by the time you use it. Obtain a per-shipment quote and check whether it matches the tier (A/B/C) and any add-ons you actually need.

## Incoterms 2020: the CIP vs CIF Detail Buyers Miss

Under **Incoterms 2020**, the seller's *minimum* insurance obligation was **raised to ICC (A) for CIP**, while **CIF keeps the lower ICC (C) minimum**. Consequence: if you buy **CIF**, the seller may only be obliged to arrange minimal (C) cover even though your car needs (A) — specify **ICC (A) explicitly in the contract/L/C**, or buy your own top-up. Under CIP the default is stronger, but still confirm the actual clause rather than assuming.

## Why the Carrier's Own Liability Is Not Enough

Carrier liability under the **Hague-Visby Rules** is capped at **666.67 SDR per package/unit or 2 SDR per kilogram (whichever is higher)** — both far below a vehicle's real value (per an insurance-brokerage source citing the convention). SDR limits are amended over time by the IMF, so confirm the current figure, but the structural point stands: **the gap between carrier cap and vehicle value is exactly what cargo insurance fills.**

## What Even "All Risks" Does Not Cover

ICC (A) is broad, not absolute. It does **not** cover mechanical/electrical breakdown, ordinary wear and tear, or **inherent vice** (the cargo's own nature). For an **EV**, the boundary around **battery/thermal (fire) events** depends on the specific policy and insurer wording — declare the EV, ask in writing whether battery fire/thermal-runaway in transit is covered or excluded, and whether dangerous-goods declaration requirements apply. Never assume an EV is covered identically to an ICE car.

## Building a Claim That Pays Out

Insurance responds to documented loss. Prepare the evidence trail before and during shipment:

- **Pre-shipment condition record**: dated photos/video of every panel, wheels, glass, interior, underside and (for EV) charge/SOC state, witnessed at handover.
- **Documents**: commercial invoice, packing list, B/L (RoRo: B/L with vehicle condition noted), insurance certificate, loading/handling receipts.
- **At discharge**: inspect **before** signing clean receipt; note damage on the tally/receipt with photos immediately; survey promptly where required.
- File within policy deadlines and preserve damaged parts/packaging for survey.

## Before You Pay Freight / Premium

- Specify **ICC (A)** in writing; add war/strikes cover if the route warrants.
- Confirm insured value = CIF × 110% and obtain a per-shipment rate (no assumed number).
- Under CIF, do not rely on the default minimum (C) — require (A) or self-insure the gap.
- For EVs, obtain written confirmation of battery-fire coverage and DG declaration duties.
- Complete a pre-shipment condition record and plan discharge inspection before signing.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Is ICC (C) enough for a shipped car?** No — it covers only major casualties; theft, scratches and loading damage, the common vehicle losses, need ICC (A).
**Why insure at 110% of CIF?** Standard practice adds 10% for expected profit and incidentals, giving a 110% insured value; premium is that amount times a per-shipment rate.
**Doesn't the shipping line pay if they damage the car?** Only up to the Hague-Visby cap (666.67 SDR/unit or 2 SDR/kg, whichever is higher) — well below vehicle value, hence separate cargo insurance.
**CIF or CIP — which gives better default cover?** Under Incoterms 2020 CIP requires at least ICC (A), while CIF defaults to only ICC (C); on CIF terms, demand ICC (A) explicitly.
**Is an EV battery fire covered by All-Risks?** It depends on the specific policy wording — declare the EV and obtain written confirmation rather than assuming.
