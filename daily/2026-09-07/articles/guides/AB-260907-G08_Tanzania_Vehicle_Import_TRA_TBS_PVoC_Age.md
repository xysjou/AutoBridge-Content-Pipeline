Tanzania is a single-country market with a deliberately two-track import system, and a vehicle exporter who treats it as one process loses time at the port. One track belongs to the **Tanzania Revenue Authority (TRA)** — customs declaration, duty assessment and clearance under the East African Community Customs Management Act. The other belongs to the **Tanzania Bureau of Standards (TBS)** — pre-shipment conformity assessment that decides whether a regulated shipment, used motor vehicles included, is even allowed in. This guide is specific to Tanzania (it is not an East-Africa-wide statement) and is built on current TBS and TRA primary pages. It explains both tracks authoritatively and, where the official primary documents for used-car age and vehicle duty rates were not retrievable at review, it states those as items to verify rather than inventing numbers.

## Track one — TBS: PVoC and the Certificate of Conformity

TBS operates the Pre-Export Verification of Conformity (**PVoC**) programme under section **4(1)(s) of the Standards Act No.2 of 2009**. The mechanism is front-loaded: every PVoC-regulated consignment must obtain a **Certificate of Conformity (CoC) before the goods are shipped to Tanzania**. TBS states plainly that regulated goods arriving at Tanzanian ports without the CoC are **rejected or penalised**. The actual pre-shipment work — physical inspection, laboratory testing where relevant, document review and, where necessary, factory inspection — is carried out by TBS-authorised inspection bodies.

### Used motor vehicles are a dedicated PVoC category

Crucially for vehicle exporters, TBS does not fold cars into the general-goods list. The TBS imports/exports control page maintains a **separate PVoC procedure and regulated HS-code list for used motor vehicles**, together with worldwide contact lists of PVoC partners specifically for used motor vehicles and contracted service zones. The named PVoC service providers include **CCIC (with a China contact list), Intertek, SGS and TÜV Rheinland**. For a China-based shipper this is operationally useful: the China-side pre-shipment inspection for a used vehicle destined for Tanzania is routed through the authorised provider's China network before sailing, not arranged after arrival.

Where a regulated consignment nonetheless arrives without a CoC, TBS provides a **Destination Inspection (D1)** route, accompanied by **penalties for un-inspected used motor vehicles** under the programme — i.e. fixing it at destination is possible but deliberately more costly and slower than doing PVoC correctly up front. Exemption categories exist and are defined by TBS; a vehicle should not be assumed exempt without checking the current exemption criteria.

## Track two — TRA: customs clearance under EACCMA and TANCIS

According to TRA's official import-procedures guidance, imports are cleared under the **East African Community Customs Management Act (EACCMA) 2004**. The practical sequence is:

1. The importer appoints a **licensed Clearing and Forwarding Agent (CFA)**.
2. Declaration is made online through the **Tanzania Customs Integrated System (TANCIS)** (Mainland and Zanzibar), and agents are asked to lodge documents **at least seven days before the goods arrive**.
3. The import document set includes the final invoice, the agent's authorisation, transport documents (B/L, AWB or road consignment note), packing list, any exemption documents, and — explicitly — **import permits from other government agencies, TBS named among them**.

That last point is where the two tracks meet: the TBS CoC is not optional parallel paperwork; it is among the permits the customs file expects. TRA separately runs **motor-vehicle registration** (change of ownership and related registration matters), which is a distinct, post-clearance step rather than the import-duty process itself.

## FAQ
**Which authority handles conformity and which handles duty?**

TBS handles pre-shipment conformity (PVoC/CoC); TRA handles customs declaration, duty assessment and clearance, and separately vehicle registration.

**Is a CoC really required before shipping?**

Yes. Under TBS PVoC, regulated consignments — used motor vehicles are a dedicated category — must hold a CoC before shipment; arriving without one means rejection or penalty, with a costlier destination-inspection route.

**Who performs the pre-shipment inspection in China?**

TBS-authorised PVoC providers include CCIC (with a China list), Intertek, SGS and TÜV Rheinland; use the provider contracted for the relevant zone.

**What is the maximum age of a used car Tanzania accepts?**

This guide does not state a number: the dedicated used-vehicle procedure document was not retrievable at review — confirm the current age limit and its calculation basis with TBS/TRA before shipping.

**How much import tax will a specific vehicle pay?**

No fixed rate is given here; classify the exact HS code and use the current TRA tariff line/motor-vehicle calculator for the actual vehicle.
