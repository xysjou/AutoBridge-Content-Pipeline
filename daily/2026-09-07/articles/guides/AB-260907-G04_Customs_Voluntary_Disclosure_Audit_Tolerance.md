There is a meaningful difference, in Chinese customs enforcement, between an error the authority finds during an audit and an error an enterprise finds and reports itself. The voluntary disclosure (主动披露) mechanism is built around that difference: an enterprise that discovers it underpaid duty or breached a customs requirement through its own internal check may submit a written report and accept handling, and — where the statutory conditions are met — receive more tolerant treatment than it would if the same problem were detected by customs. This guide sets out the legal basis, the current time-limited tolerance window, how the mechanism actually runs, and the limits that decide whether a given case qualifies.

## The legal basis

Voluntary disclosure is grounded in the Customs Audit Regulations (海关稽查条例) and their implementing rules, including Article 27 of the implementing measures, which frame the treatment of an enterprise that voluntarily reports a non-compliance found through self-inspection. On top of that standing framework, customs has issued dedicated announcements that set out, for a defined period, more concrete tolerance treatment — including circumstances in which an administrative penalty is not imposed or is reduced. The current operative announcement is time-limited, which is the single most important fact to check before relying on it.

## The current tolerance window is time-limited

The favourable treatment in the current announcement applies within a defined window: **from 11 October 2025 to 30 June 2027**. Because the window is explicit, an exporter evaluating an old error cannot assume the same terms will be available after it closes; post-window policy is not something this article predicts. Any planning should be dated against the announcement actually in force when the disclosure is submitted, and the closing date should be treated as a hard planning constraint rather than a soft target.

## What voluntary disclosure requires

The mechanism is not a verbal heads-up. Its shape is:

1. **Self-discovery through an internal check.** The enterprise identifies the problem itself — a misclassified HS code, an under-declared customs value, a missed licence requirement, a reporting omission — rather than being prompted by an open customs investigation into the same matter.
2. **A written report to customs.** The enterprise submits a written voluntary disclosure describing the facts, the period involved, the cause and the quantified impact.
3. **Acceptance of handling and make-good.** The enterprise cooperates and pays the duty (and import import-stage taxes where applicable) that should have been paid.
4. **Tolerance assessment against the conditions.** Customs assesses whether the statutory and announcement conditions for non-penalty or reduced/lighter treatment are met.

Where the conditions are satisfied, the mechanism provides that an administrative penalty may be **not imposed**, or may be **lighter or reduced**, and that late-payment surcharges may be reduced within the terms of the current rules. That is genuine leniency, but it is conditional — it is not an automatic amnesty.

## Where the leniency ends

Three boundaries matter. First, voluntary disclosure addresses violations that qualify under the rules; it does not erase the underlying obligation to pay the duty actually owed. Second, whether a specific case receives no penalty, a reduced penalty, or a surcharge reduction is determined case by case against the conditions — this guide does not promise an outcome or quote a fixed reduction ratio. Third, timing relative to customs' own knowledge is decisive: once customs has already launched an investigation or audit into the same conduct, the room to claim "voluntary" disclosure narrows sharply. Acting early is the whole point.

For a vehicle or parts exporter, the recurring candidates are classification drift across a parts catalogue, royalty or assists not reflected in customs value, origin-document gaps, and discrepancies between the declaration and the books. Each is far cheaper to self-correct with a complete file than to explain after a customs audit notice arrives.

## FAQ
**What is customs voluntary disclosure?**

It is a mechanism where an enterprise finds a customs non-compliance through self-inspection, reports it in writing, accepts handling and pays what is owed, potentially receiving no or reduced administrative penalty where conditions are met.

**What is the current tolerance window?**

The current operative announcement applies from 11 October 2025 to 30 June 2027; confirm against the announcement in force at submission, since later treatment is not assumed.

**Does voluntary disclosure waive the unpaid duty?**

No. The duty and import-stage taxes owed must still be paid; the leniency concerns administrative penalty and, within the rules, late-payment surcharges.

**Is non-penalty guaranteed?**

No. It is assessed case by case against the statutory conditions; this guide does not promise an outcome or a fixed reduction ratio.

**Does timing matter?**

Decisively. Disclosure is strongest before customs has started its own investigation or audit into the same conduct.
