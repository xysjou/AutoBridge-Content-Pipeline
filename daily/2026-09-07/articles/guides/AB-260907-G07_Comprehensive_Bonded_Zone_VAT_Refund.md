A comprehensive bonded zone (综合保税区) is not simply a warehouse with a fence; it is a special customs-supervision area where the tax treatment of goods changes according to which "line" they cross and, for qualified enterprises, whether the zone operator holds VAT general-taxpayer status. For an automotive exporter using a zone for storage, light processing, knock-down assembly or distribution, the financial difference between getting the mechanics right and wrong is substantial. This guide explains the first-line/second-line logic, why domestic goods entering a zone can be treated as exports for VAT refund, what the general-taxpayer pilot unlocks, equipment relief and selective tariffs — without naming which specific parks are currently piloted (that list is time-sensitive) or computing any tax burden for a particular case.

## The "first line" and "second line" logic

Bonded-zone operation is organised around two boundaries:

- **First line (between the zone and overseas).** Goods entering the zone from abroad enter under **bonded** treatment; the zone is, in customs-tax terms, treated as not yet inside the domestic market.
- **Second line (between the zone and the domestic Chinese market).** When goods move from the zone into the domestic market, they are declared according to their actual status and the applicable import taxes are then settled.

The corollary that matters to exporters: **domestic (Chinese) goods moving into the comprehensive bonded zone are treated as exports**, which is what makes an **entry-based VAT refund (入区退税)** possible for qualifying movements. In effect, a Chinese manufacturer can place goods into the zone and trigger export-style treatment without the goods physically leaving China yet — a mechanism widely used for consolidation before actual overseas shipment.

## The VAT general-taxpayer pilot: why it matters

A pure bonded-zone enterprise historically could only run bonded business, which limited its ability to buy/sell domestically and to issue normal VAT invoices. The **VAT general-taxpayer pilot** changes that. An enterprise that qualifies and is approved may:

- carry on **both bonded and non-bonded** business;
- purchase and sell in the domestic market under normal VAT mechanics; and
- treat goods that physically leave the territory as exports eligible for the usual export refund/exemption treatment.

This dual-track capability is what makes a zone practical for an exporter that simultaneously serves overseas orders and domestic after-sales or replacement demand. Qualification is not automatic: an enterprise must meet the pilot conditions and be approved, and **whether a particular park currently hosts the pilot is governed by a time-sensitive list** that must be checked for the actual zone rather than assumed.

## Imported self-use equipment relief

For pilot enterprises, **self-use equipment imported into the zone within the prescribed scope** can be temporarily relieved of import taxes under the policy. The qualifiers matter: the equipment must be for the enterprise's own use, it must fall within the defined scope, and the relief is tied to pilot status. Production-line tooling and certain testing equipment for zone-based processing are the typical candidates; goods for resale or equipment outside the prescribed scope do not qualify.

## Selective tariff on domestic sales

When bonded goods are sold into the domestic market, the enterprise may — within the policy and subject to customs handling — choose the tariff basis: levy duty by reference to the **imported materials/inputs** or by reference to the **goods as presented at filing (报验状态)** (cross-checked across the State Council and customs policy texts). This "selective tariff" option lets a business pick the lawful basis that matches its actual situation, but it is an election made under the rules for each domestic-sale movement, not a free choice to mix bases across transactions.

## FAQ
**Why can goods get a VAT refund just by entering a bonded zone?**

Domestic goods moving into a comprehensive bonded zone are treated as exports, which underpins the entry-based VAT refund for qualifying movements, before actual overseas departure.

**What is the first line versus the second line?**

The first line is the zone-to-overseas boundary (bonded on entry); the second line is the zone-to-domestic-market boundary, where taxes are settled according to goods' status.

**What does the VAT general-taxpayer pilot add?**

It lets an approved enterprise run both bonded and non-bonded business, transact domestically under normal VAT mechanics, and claim export refund/exemption on physical export.

**Is every bonded zone a general-taxpayer pilot?**

No — pilot parks are named on a current, time-sensitive list; verify the specific park before planning around dual-track status.

**How does selective tariff work on domestic sales?**

Subject to the rules, duty may be levied by reference either to imported inputs or to the goods as presented at filing, elected per lawful domestic-sale movement.
