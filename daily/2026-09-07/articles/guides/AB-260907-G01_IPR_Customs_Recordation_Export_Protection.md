When a Chinese-made vehicle or a container of spare parts carries a brand name, a logo, a patented mechanism or licensed design material, the goods do not clear the border in an intellectual-property vacuum. China runs a dedicated border-protection regime under the Regulations on Customs Protection of Intellectual Property Rights and their implementing rules (General Administration of Customs of China Order No.183). The decisive instrument for a rights holder is customs recordation: once a trademark, patent or copyright is recorded with GACC, customs is positioned to act at the import and export stage. This guide separates what the regime reliably does, the two routes into protection, and the points an exporter or brand owner must verify case by case — without fixing fees or bond amounts, which are assessed per matter and change over time.

## Which rights the border regime covers

The customs IPR protection regime covers three families of rights: exclusive trademark rights, patent rights, and copyright (including related rights). For an automotive exporter this maps directly onto real cargo: word and device trademarks on grilles, wheels and packaging; utility-model or design patents on lamps, trim and mechanical parts; and copyright in software, manuals and media loaded into a vehicle. A right that exists domestically is not automatically enforced at the border — the regime works most strongly where the right has first been recorded with customs.

The national IP registration side sits with the China National Intellectual Property Administration (CNIPA) for trademarks and patents, while border enforcement sits with customs. That division matters: a trademark certificate establishes the right; recordation with GACC is what arms the border mechanism. Exporters should not treat an IP certificate and a customs recordation as the same document.

## Two protection routes — and why recordation decides which one is available

The Regulations create two distinct routes, and confusing them is the most common planning error.

1. **Ex-officio protection (依职权保护).** Where a right is already recorded, customs may actively inspect consignments it suspects infringe, suspend release and detain suspect goods on its own initiative, then notify the rights holder. This proactive route presupposes a valid recordation on file; without one, customs has no recorded basis to initiate this route for that right.
2. **Protection upon application (依申请保护).** For a specific consignment, a rights holder may apply to customs to detain suspected infringing goods, even outside the standing ex-officio track. This is shipment-specific and depends on the application and supporting evidence for that batch.

The practical consequence is structural: recordation is the gateway to proactive, repeatable border protection across many shipments, whereas an on-request application solves a single identified batch. A brand owner that expects ongoing exports should record first rather than file one detention application after another.

## Where an exporter or rights holder fits in the process

The recordation process runs from the rights holder, not from the freight forwarder. The holder submits the recordation through the customs IPR recordation system with proof of the underlying right and holder identity; customs reviews and records eligible rights; thereafter the recorded right can be invoked at ports. When customs identifies a suspect shipment under the ex-officio route it suspends release and notifies the holder, who must respond within the statutory window with an application and security for the matter to proceed to detention and a determination.

For a vehicle exporter that is also a legitimate licensee, the discipline runs in both directions. Outbound, you must be able to show that every brand, badge and licensed technology on the vehicle or in its parts is authorised, so that your own cargo is not suspended as infringing. Inbound or in transit, a recorded brand you own or license can be defended against copy parts. Keep the trademark registration, the licence chain and the customs recordation aligned to the exact legal entity named on the export invoice — mismatched holder names are a frequent cause of delay.

## What customs decides — and what it does not

Customs enforces the border: it may suspend release and detain suspect goods and handle the procedural consequences under the Regulations. Customs is not the forum that finally decides whether an IP right is valid or whether infringement exists on the merits; that substantive determination belongs with the IP authorities or the courts. An exporter should therefore not read a detention as a final infringement ruling, and a rights holder should not treat recordation as a substitute for a strong underlying registration.

The security or bond a party must post, and any recordation fee, are fixed by the current official provisions and assessed for the specific matter. They are time-sensitive and case-specific; obtain the current figure from the official customs channel for the actual case rather than copying a number seen online.

## FAQ
**Does a Chinese trademark certificate automatically protect my goods at export?**

No. The certificate establishes the right with the IP authority; proactive ex-officio customs protection additionally requires the right to be recorded with GACC.

**What is the difference between ex-officio and on-request protection?**

Ex-officio action is customs-initiated and requires prior recordation; an on-request application is filed by the rights holder for one specific suspect consignment.

**Which IP types can be recorded?**

Exclusive trademark rights, patent rights and copyright and related rights, each supported by the underlying proof of right.

**Does customs finally rule on infringement?**

Customs handles the border measures such as suspension and detention; the substantive validity and infringement question is decided by the competent IP authority or the court.

**Are recordation fees and bonds fixed numbers?**

They are set by current official provisions and assessed per matter and are time-sensitive; confirm the current amount through the official customs channel for each case.
