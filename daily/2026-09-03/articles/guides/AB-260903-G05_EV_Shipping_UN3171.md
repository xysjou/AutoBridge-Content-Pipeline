## Start Here: an EV Is Dangerous Goods, Even When It Looks Like a Car
A vehicle powered by a lithium battery is classified for sea transport under **UN3171 "battery-powered vehicle/equipment," within IMDG Code Class 9** (miscellaneous dangerous goods). A hybrid carrying a traction battery is treated on the same logic. Treating an EV as ordinary RoRo/container cargo is the root cause of refused bookings and rollovers. This guide maps classification, provisions, documents and the items that change over time; the IMDG amendment in force and each carrier's extra rules must be re-confirmed at booking.

## Classification and Special Provisions
- **UN3171 / Class 9** is the entry for a battery-powered vehicle (cross-checked against Chinese maritime-safety material).
- **Special Provision 390** addresses vehicles transported with the battery installed (vehicle-and-battery together).
- **Packing Instruction P910** allows a whole vehicle to be carried **without conventional dangerous-goods packaging** (i.e., the vehicle itself is the "article"), subject to its conditions.
These provisions are why an intact, factory-built EV can ship as a vehicle rather than being crated as loose cells — but the relief is conditional, not blanket.

## The Document Set a Booking Rests On
1. **UN38.3 test report** — covering **both the cells and the battery pack/module**; a cell-only report is insufficient for the pack.
2. **GHS-compliant MSDS/SDS** — identifying **UN3171, Class 9, Packing Group III and the EmS schedule**.
3. **Dangerous-goods declaration** and carrier booking forms completed to the IMDG standard.
4. **Loose/spare batteries are a different case** — separate lithium-battery entries apply and a **dangerous-goods packaging certificate (危包证)** is required for loose batteries; do not assume the whole-vehicle relief covers spares packed alongside.
5. For **damaged or defective batteries**, separate entries such as **UN3556** apply — such units need explicit carrier approval and often cannot move at all.

## Version and Timeliness (the moving parts)
- **UN38.3 revision:** from 2026, **Revision 7 and above** is generally the accepted version (TIME_SENSITIVE — confirm the version your carrier and destination accept; older reports may be rejected).
- **China domestic rule:** a technical requirement for ships carrying lithium batteries is reported to have taken effect **1 May 2025**, refining declaration, inspection, packaging and stowage (single-source compliance analysis — confirm the current official text and how it applies to whole vehicles vs loose batteries).
- **IMDG amendment cycle:** the amendment in force changes biennially with transition periods; state the applicable amendment on the booking.

## Carrier-Specific Layer (never assume the Code is the ceiling)
On top of IMDG, shipping lines impose their own conditions, commonly including:
- A maximum **state of charge (SOC)** and proof of the 12 V/traction system state;
- Whether the vehicle may ship **by container or only RoRo**, and terminal acceptance;
- Firewatch/stowage and disconnect instructions; **damaged/recalled-battery bans**.
These vary by line and route and are TIME_SENSITIVE — obtain the carrier's current dangerous-goods acceptance in writing before promising a sailing.

## What This Guide Recommends You Prepare Before Booking
- **Cell + pack UN38.3 (current revision)** and a **GHS MSDS naming UN3171/Class 9/PG III/EmS**.
- A clear **installed-battery vs loose-battery** decision; arrange 危包证 for any spares.
- The **current IMDG amendment** and **China 2025 rule applicability** confirmed in writing.
- **Carrier SOC/stowage/route conditions** approved before container loading or RoRo delivery.
- A **damaged/defect screen** (no warning lights, no impact/flood history) — feed this into the PSI (see pre-shipment inspection guide).

## Frequently Asked Questions
**What UN number is an electric car shipped under?** UN3171 (battery-powered vehicle), IMDG Class 9, when the battery is installed in the vehicle.
**Does the EV need dangerous-goods packaging?** Under SP390/P910 a complete vehicle is not crated like loose cells, subject to conditions; loose batteries follow separate rules and need a packaging certificate.
**Is a cell-level UN38.3 report enough?** No — both cells and the battery pack/module must be covered.
**Which UN38.3 version is accepted?** Revision 7 and above is generally required from 2026; confirm with the carrier as this is time-sensitive.
**Can a damaged-EV battery ship normally?** No — damaged/defective batteries fall under separate entries (e.g., UN3556) and need explicit approval or may be refused.
