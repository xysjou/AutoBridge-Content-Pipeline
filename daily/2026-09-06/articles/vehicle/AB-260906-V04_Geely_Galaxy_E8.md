The Galaxy E8 (Yinhe E8) is Geely's flagship electric fastback sedan on the SEA (Sustainable Experience Architecture) — a five-seat, low-drag (around 0.199 Cd) battery-electric car offered in single-motor rear-drive and dual-motor all-wheel-drive forms. Because it is an EV, the purchasing decision turns on battery, test cycle, charging architecture and software, not on a single headline power number. Everything below is a Chinese-market (CLTC) reference; export versions and their charging standards require separate OEM evidence.

## Battery and CLTC range — read per trim, never as one number
Chinese sources describe several lithium battery packs (roughly 62 / 67.8 / 76 kWh across LFP and ternary lines) and CLTC ranges of 575 / 610 / 620 / 700 km attached to different battery and drive combinations. These figures are currently one independent source and the exact battery-to-range-to-trim mapping must be confirmed on the OEM configuration sheet for the actual SKU. This page deliberately does not print a fixed 'battery X = range Y' table, because doing so would assert a mapping the sources do not fully lock.
> CLTC is the Chinese test cycle. It is not WLTP or EPA; an overseas buyer should expect a different (typically lower) figure under the destination cycle, and should not compare CLTC numbers directly with WLTP-rated rivals.
## Charging architecture: 800V is version-dependent
Higher versions use an 800-volt high-voltage platform that supports higher DC fast-charging capability, while the precise peak charging power and 10–80% time are not available from an official source here and are therefore not stated as fixed values — confirm them per trim. For export, the physical connector and charging standard (GB/T in China versus CCS/other destination standards) is a separate, decisive question that an OEM export sheet must answer.
## Software, OTA and the cockpit
The E8 is built around a large integrated display and Geely's in-car operating system, with over-the-air updates. For an export buyer this raises the same diligence as any China-market EV: language availability, app-store region, map data, SIM/connectivity band, and whether OTA functions outside China. These are testable acceptance items, not assumptions — include them on the per-VIN acceptance check.
## EV-specific export verification
1. Lock the exact SKU and obtain its battery chemistry/capacity, motor layout and CLTC range from the OEM/CoC record.
2. Confirm 400V vs 800V platform for that SKU and obtain the official charging curve/peak before quoting charge time.
3. Resolve connector/standard (GB/T vs destination) and any adapter or export-build requirement.
4. Verify battery transport classification and carrier rules for the sea leg (see the IMDG/UN-classification guide).
5. Test cockpit language, connectivity band and OTA behaviour for the destination before acceptance.
6. Chinese market is LHD; steering for any export build needs separate OEM evidence.
## Quotation discipline
No export price is published and the domestic guide price is China-only and time-sensitive. Request a Current Export Quotation with Destination Country, Quantity, Preferred Trim, LHD/RHD, Destination Port and Incoterm; battery and freight must not be estimated.
## FAQ
What is the Galaxy E8’s range?
Chinese-market CLTC ranges run across 575/610/620/700 km depending on battery and drive; the exact mapping is trim-specific and must be confirmed per SKU — CLTC is not WLTP.
Is it single or dual motor?
Both: single-motor rear-drive (200 or 250 kW by version) and a dual-motor AWD performance version around 475 kW combined.
Does every E8 use 800V?
Higher versions use an 800V platform; confirm the exact SKU, and obtain official peak-charging figures rather than assuming a number.
Can I charge it on European CCS?
The Chinese car uses the GB/T standard; destination connector compatibility requires an OEM export-build answer, not an assumption.
How big is the car?
Around 5010×1920×1465 mm with a 2925 mm wheelbase (Chinese-market reference, confirm by trim).
