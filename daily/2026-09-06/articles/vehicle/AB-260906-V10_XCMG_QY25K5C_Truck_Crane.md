A truck crane such as the XCMG QY25K5C is a **special-purpose vehicle** combining a road-going truck carrier (chassis) with a rotating lifting superstructure. Sourcing one is unlike buying a truck: the decisive documents are the OEM load chart, the boom configuration and the split between carrier and crane approvals. This page identifies the QY25K5C as XCMG's 25-tonne-class machine and explains what must be obtained from official XCMG material — it deliberately does not borrow specifications from older QY25E/QY25K-II models.

## Carrier versus superstructure — the core distinction

- **Carrier (chassis)**: engine, transmission, axles, cab, GVW, road homologation and the travel configuration; subject to vehicle-type approval.
- **Superstructure (upper)**: telescopic boom sections, slewing ring/turntable, winch, outriggers, hydraulic system and the rated-capacity/load chart; governed by lifting-equipment standards.
- A truck crane is road-legal and lifting-safe through **two separate evidence chains** — approving one does not approve the other.

## Why the load chart, not the class number, sets capability

The '25-tonne' label is a nominal class. Actual rated lift at any moment depends on boom length, working radius, outrigger state and slewing angle — all defined in the OEM load chart (the lift-by-radius table). A buyer cannot infer boom length, maximum lifting height, engine power, travel dimensions or top speed from the class number, and must not copy values from adjacent older XCMG models (QY25E, QY25K-II), which are different machines.

> Main-boom sections/length, maximum lift height, engine model/power, overall dimensions and top speed for the QY25K5C specifically are NOT stated here: they require the official XCMG QY25K5C sample/specification sheet or the chassis homologation. Values from other K-series or older 25 t machines are not transferable.

## Export and pre-payment checklist

1. Request the official XCMG QY25K5C specification and the full rated-capacity load chart; confirm boom sections/length and max lift height for this exact model.
2. Obtain the carrier chassis homologation (engine, GVW, axles, dimensions, emission stage) separately from the crane-superstructure certificate.
3. Confirm destination classification of a mobile crane (road vehicle vs lifting equipment) and the operator/inspection regime.
4. Check outrigger spread, working weight and axle loads against route/bridge limits; plan heavy-lift or break-bulk shipping.
5. Verify diesel emission stage/fuel compatibility and spare-part availability for both carrier and upper works.
6. Chinese market is LHD; match chassis and superstructure serial numbers across contract, CoC and shipping documents.

## FAQ
**What does "25-tonne class" actually mean?**

It is a nominal class; the real lift at any radius/boom/outrigger state is read from the XCMG load chart, not from the class number.

**Is a truck crane one vehicle or two systems?**

It combines a road carrier chassis and a lifting superstructure, each with its own homologation/certification chain.

**Is it regulated as special equipment in China?**

The mobile truck crane has been removed from the SAMR special-equipment "lifting machinery" catalogue, though road and worksite rules still apply.

**Can I use QY25K-II or QY25E specs for the QY25K5C?**

No — those are different models; boom, engine and dimensions must come from the official QY25K5C sheet.

**What documents must the buyer obtain?**

The OEM QY25K5C spec and load chart, plus the separate carrier-chassis homologation and superstructure certification.
