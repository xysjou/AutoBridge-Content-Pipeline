The customs supervision (trade) method printed on the export declaration is not a formality — it determines how the goods are declared, whether input VAT supports an export refund, how foreign exchange is received, and which goods are even eligible. Vehicle exporters most often meet general trade, but are sometimes offered market-procurement (1039) or bonded routes as 'simpler'. This page explains each, and why a complete vehicle does not automatically qualify for the simplified mode.

## General trade (0110): the default for a proper vehicle export

General trade is the standard mode for a licensed trading company or manufacturer exporting goods it owns, declared under supervision code 0110. It requires a compliant commercial chain — purchase, export contract, customs declaration, foreign-exchange receipt — and, where the exporter is a general VAT taxpayer with valid input invoices, it is the route through which export tax refund (exemption/credit) is normally claimed. For a whole-vehicle export that also needs the vehicle export licence/quota system, general trade is the conventional, fully documented path.

## Market-procurement trade (1039): purpose-built for a different profile

China Customs defines market-procurement trade as goods purchased **within an officially recognised market cluster** and cleared by an eligible operator, declared under code 1039. GACC describes it as a new trade format rolled out in batches across recognised pilot clusters; goods are declared through the market-procurement comprehensive management system. Its design target is **small-batch, multi-variety** goods from these clusters, with simplified VAT treatment (exemption without refund under the relevant tax rule) suited to traders who cannot obtain conventional input invoices.

> Whether a **complete vehicle** may exit under 1039 is not a yes/no that applies nationwide: market clusters focus on small commodities, and eligibility plus the negative list must be confirmed with the competent local customs and the cluster operator before shipment. Do not assume 1039 is available for whole cars. The per-declaration value ceiling and pilot scope are time-sensitive — use the current published rule, not a remembered USD figure.

## Bonded supervision: goods that have not yet entered domestic circulation

Bonded/特殊监管区域 routes (e.g. comprehensive bonded zones) concern goods kept under customs bond — entered, stored, processed or re-exported without the normal import-duty/VAT step. They fit distribution, simple processing or entrepôt models (a regional vehicle hub serving multiple destinations, for example) rather than a straightforward domestic-purchase export. Moving a Chinese-built vehicle through a bonded zone changes the document and tax chain and should be designed with the zone and customs in advance.

## A decision path that protects the vehicle deal

1. Identify the goods: a complete licensed vehicle, parts, or accessories — they do not share the same eligibility.
2. Confirm the seller's VAT status and whether valid input invoices exist; this decides whether refund logic even applies.
3. If 1039 is proposed, verify in writing with the **competent customs of the cluster** that the exact product is not on the excluded list and that the per-entry limit and pilot scope currently apply.
4. Use bonded routes only where a genuine bonded operation exists; do not route a normal domestic sale through a zone for convenience.
5. Align the trade mode with the export licence, FX receipt (see RMB settlement and credit-insurance guides) and tax filing so all documents tell the same story.

## Modes that create downstream damage

- Mismatching the supervision code to the actual transaction, which invites customs challenge; using 1039 to dodge input-invoice or licensing requirements for an ineligible whole vehicle; quoting the per-entry ceiling or pilot list from an outdated source; splitting a bonded and a domestic flow without the zone paperwork.

## FAQ
**What is supervision code 0110?**

It is general trade, the standard mode for a normal B2B export and the conventional path for documented whole-vehicle exports.

**What is 1039 market-procurement trade?**

Goods bought in an officially recognised market cluster and declared by an eligible operator through the market-procurement system, designed for small multi-variety goods.

**Can I export a whole car under 1039?**

Not automatically — eligibility and the excluded-goods list must be confirmed with the competent local customs for the specific cluster.

**Does 1039 give an export tax refund?**

It generally follows the exemption-without-refund model; general trade with valid input invoices is the normal refund route.

**Are the 1039 value ceiling and pilot list fixed?**

No — they are updated over time and are time-sensitive; verify the current rule before declaring.
