Finished vehicles shipped by RoRo may carry little wood, but knocked-down units, spare parts, CKD/SKD kits and containerised vehicles rely on crates, pallets, chocks and dunnage — and almost every country regulates that wood under the IPPC's ISPM-15. A missing or invalid mark is one of the most avoidable causes of re-export, fumigation-on-arrival or delay. This page explains what ISPM-15 requires, how the mark is built, and how the China-side treatment and exit registration works.
## What ISPM-15 regulates and why
ISPM-15 (Regulation of wood packaging material in international trade), maintained by the International Plant Protection Convention under the FAO, reduces the risk of moving forest pests in raw wood used to support or brace cargo. It covers solid-wood packaging such as crates, boxes, pallets, dunnage and spacers; processed wood materials (e.g. certain plywood/particle board) and very thin wood fall outside its scope as defined in the standard. The obligation applies to coniferous and non-coniferous solid wood alike.
## Approved treatments — use the current annex
The standard recognises approved measures identified by treatment codes, principally conventional heat treatment (HT) and methyl bromide (MB) fumigation, with dielectric heating (DH) recognised under defined conditions. The required wood-core temperature, duration and MB schedules are specified in the current ISPM-15 annex and its revisions.
> Do not quote a treatment temperature, holding time or dosage from memory. ISPM-15 has been revised (including DH provisions); read the current annex for the precise schedule, and note some destinations restrict or ban methyl bromide for environmental reasons.
## Reading the mark — four required components
Treated wood packaging must carry a specific, durable mark showing, per the IPPC text:
- The IPPC symbol;
- A two-letter country code;
- A unique producer/treatment-provider code assigned by the national plant protection organisation;
- The treatment code (e.g. HT, DH or MB) using the approved abbreviation.
The mark must appear on opposite sides in a legible, permanent and non-transferable way. A handwritten note, a supplier's invoice line or a sticker that is not the prescribed mark does not satisfy ISPM-15. If the mark is absent, illegible, or belongs to wood that has been repaired with untreated timber, the consignment can fail at the destination.
## China-side: who is allowed to treat and mark
On the export side, China Customs administers the supervision of exit wood-packaging treatment and the registration of treatment enterprises: an enterprise that heat-treats or fumigates exit wood packaging and applies the mark must be qualified/registered with customs, and the packaging must be treated and marked under that regime. Exporters should therefore source treated wood packaging from a customs-registered treatment provider and retain its treatment certificate — not buy unmarked pallets and treat them informally.
## Destination layer: ISPM-15 is the floor, not the ceiling
National plant protection organisations implement ISPM-15 and may add requirements. The United States APHIS program is one example of a detailed national WPM regime; other markets may require an additional fumigation certificate, specific declarations, or impose stricter MB rules. The international standard sets the common baseline; the destination authority sets the extra conditions.

## Failure modes that cause destination problems
- Unmarked or only-invoice-declared wood; bark residue beyond the allowed limits; a mark from a different shipment reused on new wood; mixing treated and untreated timber after a repack; assuming the ISPM baseline satisfies a country that demands more.
## FAQ
Does ISPM-15 apply to the car itself or the wood around it?
It regulates solid-wood packaging — crates, pallets, dunnage and bracing — not the vehicle, but CKD/parts and container bracing often contain regulated wood.
What must the mark contain?
The IPPC symbol, a country code, a unique producer/treatment-provider code and the treatment code such as HT or MB.
Can I heat-treat and stamp pallets myself?
In China, exit wood-packaging treatment and marking must be done by a customs-registered qualified treatment enterprise.
Is HT always accepted and MB banned?
HT is widely accepted; MB is an ISPM-recognised option but some destinations restrict it — confirm the destination position.
Does ISPM-15 compliance guarantee entry?
It sets the international floor; national authorities may add certificate or declaration requirements that must also be met.
