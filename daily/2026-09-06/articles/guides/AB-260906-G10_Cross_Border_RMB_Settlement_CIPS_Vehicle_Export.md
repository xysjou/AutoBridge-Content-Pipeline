Invoicing a vehicle export in renminbi instead of US dollars removes one layer of exchange risk for the Chinese seller — but only if the settlement is legally structured, the buyer can actually obtain and pay RMB, and the banking channel supports it. This page explains the policy basis for cross-border RMB settlement, how a vehicle exporter receives RMB, the export tax-refund position, and precisely what the Cross-Border Interbank Payment System (CIPS) does and does not do.

## Eligibility: RMB settlement is a normal, policy-supported option

The legal foundation is the cross-border trade RMB settlement framework established by the People's Bank of China and partner ministries (the 2009 pilot administrative measures and their implementing rules). Under it, enterprises that declare and settle goods trade in RMB use the cross-border RMB channel; the pilot was broadened over time so that legitimate current-account trade — including vehicle exports — can be settled in renminbi through settlement banks. Eligibility is about operating through a qualified bank with a true trade background, not about a special licence for each deal.

## How the money actually moves

1. The sales contract and proforma invoice denominate in RMB, and the export declaration is consistent with RMB settlement.
2. The overseas buyer arranges RMB payment through its bank (an overseas participating/agent bank).
3. Funds clear through the cross-border RMB clearing infrastructure — via an RMB clearing bank or **CIPS** — to the Chinese exporter's settlement bank.
4. The Chinese bank verifies the trade background (contract, declaration, receipt consistency) and credits RMB to the enterprise account.

## Tax refund: RMB settlement does not forfeit the export refund

A common worry is that receiving RMB rather than foreign exchange blocks the export tax refund. The implementing framework treats legitimate RMB trade settlement as eligible for export rebate treatment on the same basis as foreign-exchange settlement, provided the trade background and documents are compliant. The refund turns on valid input invoices and a proper general-trade chain (see the trade-modes guide), not on the settlement currency itself. Confirm the operational documents with the tax authority and settlement bank for the specific case.

## What CIPS is — and what it is not

CIPS is a financial-market infrastructure that provides clearing and settlement of cross-border and offshore RMB payments for its **participating institutions**, under PBoC business rules (the current CIPS Business Rules define its operators and participants). It is the interbank pipe: it moves RMB between banks. It is **not** a party to the trade contract, not a lender to the exporter, and not an escrow service; an exporter does not 'open a CIPS account' — it banks with a settlement bank that reaches CIPS or an RMB clearing arrangement. The current participant directory is published by CIPS and changes, so do not hard-code a correspondent relationship.

## The real-world constraint: can the buyer pay RMB?

> Policy permission on the Chinese side does not guarantee RMB liquidity on the buyer side. Whether the destination market supports RMB payment, whether the buyer's bank can route it, and at what cost, must be confirmed case by case with the buyer and the banks. Settlement fees and arrival times vary by bank and are time-sensitive; obtain them rather than assuming numbers.

## RMB versus FX, and where SAFE sits

Foreign-exchange receipt is governed by the State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE); cross-border RMB runs under the PBoC RMB framework. Choosing RMB moves the transaction out of the FX-conversion track but does not exempt it from trade-background verification, anti-money-laundering checks or sanctions screening. For the exporter, the cleanest structure aligns contract currency, declaration currency, receipt currency and tax filing so no document contradicts another.

## FAQ
**Can a Chinese vehicle exporter legally receive RMB for an export?**

Yes — legitimate goods trade can be settled in RMB through the cross-border RMB channel via a qualified settlement bank under the PBoC framework.

**Does RMB settlement block the export tax refund?**

No; compliant RMB trade settlement supports export rebate treatment on the same basis, subject to normal invoice and trade-chain requirements.

**Do I open an account with CIPS?**

No — CIPS clears between participating banks; an exporter uses its settlement bank, which connects to CIPS or an RMB clearing bank.

**Does CIPS guarantee payment or act as escrow?**

No, it is interbank payment infrastructure, not a trade party, lender or escrow agent.

**Why might an overseas buyer not pay in RMB?**

Limited local RMB liquidity or bank routing in some markets; confirm capability and cost case by case.
