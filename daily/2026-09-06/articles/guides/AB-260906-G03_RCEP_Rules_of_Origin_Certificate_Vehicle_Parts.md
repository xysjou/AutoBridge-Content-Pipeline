A vehicle or a container of auto parts only receives an RCEP preferential tariff at the destination if it **qualifies as originating** and is **documented in a way the importing customs accepts**. Being shipped from China is not enough, and a commercial invoice saying 'made in China' is not a proof of origin. This page walks through the RCEP origin tests, the role of regional cumulation, the three proof routes, and the back-to-back mechanism that matters when goods move through a third RCEP party.

## First test: does the good actually originate?

RCEP gives two basic ways a good can be originating: it is **wholly obtained or produced** in a party (largely irrelevant for finished vehicles), or it is produced **exclusively from originating materials**, or it meets the **Product-Specific Rule (PSR)** for its exact HS code — typically a Regional Value Content (RVC) threshold, a tariff-classification change (CTC), or a specified process. The applicable rule is read from the RCEP product-specific rules annex **by the precise HS code**, not guessed from the product name.

> Do not apply a remembered RVC percentage to a vehicle or a part. Chapter 87 vehicles and Chapter 84/85 components carry different PSRs; look up the exact tariff line in the current annex.

## Regional cumulation — the advantage for an auto supply chain

RCEP cumulation lets originating materials from one RCEP party count toward the origin of a good finished in another party, subject to the agreement's conditions. For a Chinese vehicle or part that uses components sourced from RCEP partners, cumulation can lift the RVC that a purely bilateral calculation would miss. The materials must themselves be originating and documented; mere transit through a party does not add origin.

## Three ways to prove origin to destination customs

| Proof route | Who issues / makes it | When it fits a vehicle exporter |
|---|---|---|
| Certificate of Origin (Form RCEP) by a competent authority | China Customs / authorised certifying body | The standard route for most shippers; apply per shipment with supporting BOM and cost data. |
| Declaration of Origin by an Approved Exporter | The approved exporter itself | Enterprises authorised under domestic procedures may self-declare; authorisation and conditions are set by China Customs. |
| Declaration of Origin by producer/exporter (where permitted) | Producer/exporter for qualifying consignments | Some parties allow declarations under agreed thresholds/arrangements — confirm the importing party accepts it for that shipment. |

A proof that the destination customs does not accept cannot be repaired after arrival without cost and delay, so confirm the accepted format for the specific importing party before the goods leave.

## Back-to-back certificates for split or transiting consignments

When an RCEP-originating consignment passes through an intermediate RCEP party — for example parts split or re-consolidated in a regional hub — the intermediate customs may issue a **back-to-back certificate of origin** based on the original proof, preserving the originating status for the final destination, provided the goods remain under customs control and do not undergo operations beyond preservation. Auto-parts consolidators shipping multiple buyers from a regional warehouse rely on exactly this mechanism; breaking customs control or adding non-originating processing can forfeit preference.

## Building the origin file for a vehicle or part

1. Classify the finished good to its exact HS code and pull its PSR from the current RCEP annex.
2. Map the Bill of Materials, tagging each input's source and originating status so cumulation and RVC are defensible.
3. Compute RVC on a consistent formula (the agreement specifies the build-up/build-down methods) — keep cost worksheets that customs can audit.
4. Choose the proof route the destination accepts and obtain/make it before export; align invoice, packing list, B/L and certificate values.
5. For trans-shipment or splitting, plan back-to-back documentation and keep goods under customs control.

## Where preference is lost — and how to avoid it

- Using an average or borrowed RVC instead of the line-specific PSR.
- Treating non-originating assembly in a transit country as cumulation.
- Mismatch between the certificate HS/value and the commercial documents.
- Assuming the preferential rate without checking the partner's current tariff schedule for that year — RCEP tariff elimination is phased and differs by party.
- Letting the certificate lapse after the agreed validity period or splitting shipments without back-to-back paperwork.

The actual preferential duty for a specific vehicle or part in a given RCEP market is set by that party's current schedule and is time-sensitive; confirm it with the destination customs or importer rather than stating a fixed concession.

## FAQ
**Is a China certificate of origin enough for RCEP preference?**

A Form RCEP certificate issued by the competent authority is one accepted route; an approved-exporter declaration may also work where the importing party accepts it.

**What is regional cumulation in practice?**

Originating inputs from RCEP parties can be counted toward the origin of goods finished in another party, subject to the rules and documentation.

**Where do I find the RVC for a vehicle?**

In the RCEP product-specific rules annex against the exact HS code — there is no single percentage that covers all vehicles or parts.

**What is a back-to-back certificate?**

A certificate re-issued by an intermediate RCEP party based on the original proof, preserving origin for goods split or transiting under customs control.

**Does RCEP make all vehicles duty-free immediately?**

No — concessions are phased and differ by party and tariff line; verify the current schedule for the destination.
