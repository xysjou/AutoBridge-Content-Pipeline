Kenya divides a vehicle import between the tax authority and the standards body, and the standards step happens largely **before the vehicle leaves the origin country**. The **Kenya Revenue Authority (KRA)** handles customs valuation, duties and clearance; the **Kenya Bureau of Standards (KEBS)** runs the Pre-Export Verification of Conformity (PVoC) programme that decides whether a regulated import meets Kenya standards. This is a Kenya-specific guide; the numbers and lists involved are time-sensitive and must be read from the current KRA/KEBS sources.

## KEBS PVoC: conformity verified before export

PVoC is a conformity-assessment programme applied in the exporting country: appointed inspection companies verify that products — including motor vehicles, mobile equipment and used spare parts, covered in a dedicated section of the KEBS PVoC Operations Manual — meet applicable Kenya standards before shipment. A Certificate of Conformity (CoC) from the appointed PVoC agent is part of the clearance documents KRA expects for regulated products; where the programme routes apply, an Import Standardization Mark (ISM) is issued following the KEBS guidelines. The current PVoC manual (version 14) names the countries and appointed agents for vehicle inspection, so the exporter should confirm which agent covers China for the actual shipment.

- Verify which KEBS-appointed inspection agent serves the origin country for vehicles.
- Arrange pre-export inspection in time to obtain the CoC before the vessel sails — a CoC cannot be reconstructed cheaply after arrival.
- Match the CoC, invoice and VIN/unit details; mismatches complicate KRA clearance.
- Where applicable, follow the ISM route described in the KEBS Import Standardization Mark guidelines.

## KRA: customs procedure and the tax stack

KRA states that motor-vehicle imports are subject not to a single import duty but to a stack that varies with HS classification — import duty plus excise, VAT, the Import Declaration Fee and the Railway Development Levy at prescribed rates, all payable before release. KRA applies its own customs valuation framework rather than accepting the invoice at face value; the precise used-vehicle valuation reference and the current figures must be read from the current KRA page for the specific unit. A licensed customs clearing agent files the declaration, supported by the CoC/ISM where applicable, invoice and other import documents.

> This page deliberately gives no fixed duty, excise, VAT, IDF or RDL percentages and no CRSP formula. They are revised by KRA and depend on engine class, age and HS code; the importer must use KRA's current published rates and valuation for the specific unit.

## The vehicle-age rule is rolling — read the current KRA statement

Kenya controls the age of imported used vehicles, and KRA publishes the allowed manufacture years on a rolling basis (its customs FAQ states the manufacture years currently permitted). Because that cut-off moves forward over time, an exporter must not hard-code a fixed 'eight-year' or any other number into a sales pitch; the correct action is to check the current KRA statement for the year of shipment and compare it against the specific unit's manufacture date. Kenya's steering-side and road-safety/emissions expectations should likewise be confirmed against the current KRA/KEBS position for the specific unit rather than assumed.

## Order of operations for a China–Kenya vehicle deal

1. Confirm the unit meets Kenya steering/age expectations against the **current** KRA position before accepting the order.
2. Book the KEBS-appointed pre-export inspection and obtain the PVoC CoC in the origin country.
3. Prepare invoice, packing/unit list and B/L consistent with the CoC and VIN.
4. Have the Kenyan importer's clearing agent file KRA entry and settle the current duty stack on KRA valuation.
5. Obtain ISM where the PVoC route requires, then complete clearance and registration.

## Where China-side exporters lose the deal

- Quoting a fixed age limit or duty that has since changed; shipping before the PVoC CoC exists; offering a unit whose steering side or manufacture year the current KRA rules do not admit; mismatching the CoC and invoice; assuming a clean Chinese inspection substitutes for the KEBS-appointed PVoC.

## FAQ
**Does KEBS inspection happen in China or Kenya?**

PVoC is a pre-export programme performed in the exporting country by a KEBS-appointed agent; the CoC then supports Kenya clearance.

**Is import duty a single percentage?**

No — KRA applies duty plus excise, VAT, import declaration and railway levies that vary by HS, engine and age; use current KRA figures.

**What is the maximum age of an imported used car?**

It is a rolling manufacture-year rule published by KRA; check the current allowed years at shipment rather than using a fixed number.

**What is an ISM?**

The Import Standardization Mark issued under KEBS guidelines for imports routed through the PVoC programme where applicable.

**Can the exporter clear the car directly?**

A licensed Kenyan clearing agent typically lodges customs entry for the importer of record; the exporter supplies conforming documents and the CoC.
