## Why a Missing Paper Outweighs a Missing Option
A vehicle can be repaired at destination; a missing or mismatched document often cannot be fixed remotely, and it blocks registration. The exporter's job is to hand over a **complete, internally consistent paper set** in which the VIN is identical on every document (see the VIN/nameplate guide). This page covers the **Chinese issuing side**; whether a destination accepts a Chinese CoC for its own registration is that country's decision and is not assumed here.

## The Core Manufacturer Document
The **Manufacturer Certificate of Completion (机动车整车出厂合格证)** is the central proof that the vehicle left the factory conforming to standards; it is issued by the manufacturer and its format is filed with the competent authority (the GB 21085 standard framework governs the certificate). For vehicles completed from an **imported incomplete vehicle or via multi-stage manufacture**, the completion certificate is still the single whole-vehicle certificate issued at final completion; a vehicle sold only domestically carries an imported-vehicle technical parameter sheet instead.

## What the Handover Package Normally Contains
Cross-referencing government procurement delivery terms and full-process export document lists, a vehicle handover typically includes:
- Full **sales invoice**
- (Domestic-manufactured) **manufacturer certificate**
- **Commodity inspection** document where applicable
- **Certificate of conformity (CoC) / consistency certificate**
- **Warranty (three-guarantee) certificate**
- **Maintenance/service manual** and **owner's manual**
- **On-board tools and spare-parts list**

For **new-car export sales**, the domestic registration stage additionally retains the **manufacturer's safety/technical inspection certificate (original)** and a **copy of the commerce-authority motor-vehicle export licence**, per the motor-vehicle registration work rules.

## NEV Add-Ons
A new-energy vehicle export carries extra dangerous-goods paperwork (linked to the IMDG battery guide):
- **UN38.3 test summary** for the traction battery
- **Dangerous-goods packaging certificate** where applicable
- **MSDS**
These sit alongside the ordinary vehicle set; an EV delivered with only ICE-style paperwork is incomplete.

## Commercial and Special Vehicles: the Chassis Paper Trail
For trucks, tractors and upfit vehicles (see the Shunda, Tianlong KL and Tianjin KR pages), keep **chassis certificate and completed-vehicle certificate distinct**:
- The **chassis maker's** papers describe the chassis/cab/driveline.
- The **body builder's** papers describe the superstructure (e.g., reefer unit, cargo box) and the completed vehicle's GVW/payload.
A reefer or special-purpose vehicle needs both layers; a chassis certificate alone does not describe the finished truck.

## Translation and Authentication for Overseas Use
Overseas customs/registration frequently require the **CoC/spec sheet in English or the destination language, stamped by the issuing party**. Whether **notarization, consular legalization or Apostille** is additionally required depends on the destination and is checked per country — this guide does not assert a universal requirement, and it does not claim a Chinese CoC is automatically accepted as the destination's registration CoC.

## Pre-Handover Document Check
1. Same VIN across certificate, CoC, invoice and shipping papers.
2. Manufacturer certificate present and matching nameplate (GB 21085 framework).
3. NEV: UN38.3 summary + DG packaging cert + MSDS.
4. Commercial: chassis and completed-vehicle layers both present.
5. Required translated/stamped versions prepared for the destination.

## Frequently Asked Questions
**What is the most important Chinese factory document?** The manufacturer certificate of completion (整车出厂合格证), proving the vehicle conforms at factory completion.
**Do EVs need extra papers?** Yes — typically the UN38.3 test summary, a dangerous-goods packaging certificate where applicable and an MSDS.
**Is a Chinese CoC automatically accepted abroad for registration?** Not assumed — destination acceptance is a country-specific matter.
**What is different for a truck with an upfit?** Keep the chassis-maker's papers and the body builder's completed-vehicle/superstructure papers as two layers.
