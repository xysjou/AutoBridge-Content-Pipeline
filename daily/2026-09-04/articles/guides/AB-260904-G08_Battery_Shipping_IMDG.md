## The Version Question Comes First
Dangerous-goods guidance goes stale fast, and quoting a superseded UN number is a compliance failure, not a typo. The **current mandatory IMDG Code is Amendment 42-24**, adopted by IMO Resolution **MSC.556(108)**, **mandatory in China from 2026-01-01** (China Maritime Safety Administration official Chinese text). Any vehicle/battery shipping procedure written for older amendments must be re-checked against 42-24.

## Vehicles With Batteries Installed: the New UN Numbers
Under 42-24, **battery-powered vehicles transported with their batteries installed** are classified by chemistry:
| UN number | Vehicle type |
|---|---|
| **UN3556** | **Lithium-ion-battery-powered** vehicle (the mainstream BEV/PHEV case) |
| **UN3557** | **Lithium-metal-battery-powered** vehicle |
| **UN3558** | **Sodium-ion-battery-powered** vehicle |
A new **packing instruction P912** governs stowage and securing for these vehicles. Where a UN3556 vehicle is **not fully enclosed** in packaging/crates, special provision **SP405** sets the marking requirements.

## What Happened to UN3171
The old **UN3171** entry has been **narrowed in scope**: it now covers only vehicles/equipment powered by **wet batteries, sodium-metal or sodium-alloy batteries**. Mainstream **lithium vehicles must no longer be declared under UN3171** from the 2026 mandatory date. Declaring a modern BEV as UN3171 is an outdated classification and a declaration error. (UN3556 is the *installed-vehicle* entry — it is not a "damaged/defective battery" classification; damaged/defective batteries follow separate provisions.)

## Spare and Loose Batteries Are a Different Track
A traction battery shipped **separately from the vehicle** does not use the vehicle UN number:
- **UN3480** — **lithium-ion batteries shipped loose/alone**.
- **UN3481** — lithium-ion batteries **packed with, or contained in, equipment**.
Spare traction packs, service batteries and power banks follow the 3480/3481 track with their own packing/labelling, not UN3556.

## The UN38.3 Test Framework
Lithium batteries offered for transport must have passed the tests in **Section 38.3 of the UN *Manual of Tests and Criteria* (UN38.3)** and carry a **test summary** — a defined set of tests covering electrical, mechanical and environmental abuse. For an NEV export the UN38.3 summary is part of the battery paperwork that travels with the shipment (see the document-package guide). The UN Manual primary text should be cited for formal regulatory wording; this page describes the framework.

## State of Charge and Carrier Layer
The IMDG sets the regulatory floor, but **state-of-charge and quantity/condition limits also depend on the IMDG text and the individual carrier's acceptance rules**. This guide deliberately gives **no fixed SOC percentage**: an unverified universal number is exactly the kind of value that drifts between amendments and carriers. Obtain the operative SOC/condition requirement from the **carrier's booking acceptance for that voyage**, on top of 42-24. Carriers may impose stricter terms than the Code minimum; **damaged or defective** batteries have their own stricter regime.

## Declaration Workflow We Recommend
1. Identify **installed vehicle vs loose battery** → UN3556/3557/3558 vs UN3480/3481.
2. Confirm **chemistry** (lithium-ion / lithium-metal / sodium-ion).
3. Apply **P912** (and SP405 marking if not fully enclosed).
4. Attach **UN38.3 test summary** and required DG documents; MSDS where applicable.
5. Obtain the **carrier-specific** SOC/condition acceptance before booking.

## Frequently Asked Questions
**What UN number is a lithium-ion electric car under current rules?** UN3556 when transported with its lithium-ion battery installed, under IMDG 42-24.
**Can I still use UN3171 for a BEV?** No — UN3171 is narrowed to wet/sodium-metal/sodium-alloy battery vehicles; lithium vehicles use UN3556–3558.
**What about a spare battery shipped separately?** Loose lithium-ion batteries are UN3480 (or UN3481 when packed with/in equipment), not UN3556.
**Is there a universal shipping SOC?** No — apply the IMDG text plus the specific carrier's voyage acceptance; do not rely on a fixed percentage.
