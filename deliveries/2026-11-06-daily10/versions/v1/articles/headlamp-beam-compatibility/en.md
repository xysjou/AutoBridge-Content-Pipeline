# Headlamp beam compatibility before a cross-market car purchase

A seller sends a screenshot showing a setting for driving on the other side of the road. It looks reassuring: select the option, collect the car, and carry on. Yet the screenshot answers only a narrow question about a menu. It does not establish whether the fitted lamps, their operating modes and their resulting beam meet the destination's requirements for the intended import and registration route.

Headlamp suitability deserves its own purchase decision. Steering-wheel position, traffic-side adaptation, beam aim and legal acceptance are related considerations, but none is a substitute for all the others. Treating them as a single “left-hand-drive or right-hand-drive” question can leave a buyer with an apparently complete answer that does not address the actual lamps.

## Begin with the lamps the car actually has

Ask the seller to identify the fitted headlamp assemblies and the vehicle's original sales market. Request photographs of the actual lamps and available identification markings, together with the relevant manufacturer's instructions. A generic brochure for the model family is less useful if it cannot be connected to the equipment on the offered car.

This is a recommended purchasing approach, not a universal statutory document list. Its purpose is to give a competent destination specialist enough information to evaluate a specific vehicle. If the seller is showing a demonstration car rather than the stock vehicle, keep those records separate. A menu image from another trim or another model year should not silently become evidence for your purchase.

Also establish what the seller is promising. “There is a travel setting,” “a workshop can adjust the lamps,” and “the destination accepts this lamp configuration” are different statements. Ask for separate answers and the supporting material for each. A price negotiation is premature if the parties are using the word “compatible” to mean different things.

## Temporary adaptation has a purpose and a boundary

Volvo's UK manual for the V60 2021 provides a useful, specifically bounded example. Its headlamp-pattern adaptation is described for temporary use, and active bending lights are unavailable while the adaptation is in operation. That shows why a menu option should be read alongside the instructions describing its purpose and consequences.

It does not prove that another Volvo, a different model year or another manufacturer's vehicle behaves the same way. Nor does a temporary-use feature establish acceptance for permanent importation. For the proposed car, ask which manual applies, what the selected mode changes, whether any lighting functions become unavailable and whether the manufacturer describes the setting as temporary.

If a seller responds with an assurance that software will solve the issue, request the applicable written instruction rather than accepting a general capability claim. The question is not whether software can change something; it is whether the documented configuration is suitable for the intended use and acceptable in the destination. Keep that distinction even when the menu itself is easy to operate.

## Aim is a technical result, not an import decision

HELLA's workshop explanation of headlight adjustment stresses correct setup and manufacturer instructions. It also notes that some systems require a diagnostic basic setting. This makes a casual wall photograph an incomplete basis for a buying decision: without knowing how the vehicle and lamps were prepared, the image does not establish a properly assessed result.

Commission an appropriately qualified workshop to describe what it checked, which instructions it followed and what configuration was assessed. Ask whether diagnostic preparation was relevant to that system. Avoid prescribing universal adjustment distances, angles or retrofit steps from an online buying guide; those details belong to the applicable procedure and competent workshop.

Even a well-documented adjustment does not answer every regulatory question. A technical report can describe the lamps and the outcome of work. It cannot, merely by existing, establish that a registration authority accepts the vehicle under the intended import category. Send the report to the relevant specialist or authority as part of the acceptance question, rather than treating “adjusted” as synonymous with “approved.”

## Read each jurisdiction's example within its limits

Great Britain's MOT inspection manual for passenger and light commercial vehicles includes lamp condition and correct light output. That is a defined inspection context, not a universal statement about importing vehicles. A claim that a car can pass an MOT should therefore not be expanded into a promise that all destination import requirements are satisfied.

Northern Ireland's headlamp-aim guidance accepts temporary masks or converters only where the aim is correct, light output is not unduly reduced and the equipment is secure. These conditions matter together. A seller who mentions only the existence of masks has not answered whether the resulting arrangement satisfies those conditions. The guidance is not a permanent-import solution for every destination.

Northern Ireland's separate headlamp guidance also addresses a matched pair and the operation of a fitted levelling system. This broadens the buyer's attention beyond a single beam photograph: the complete installed arrangement matters within that testing context. It does not justify inventing an identical rule for every country or every category of vehicle.

Australia's Northern Territory gives another narrowly defined example. Its safety requirements for personally imported light vehicles list headlights deflecting down and left. This is evidence for that particular jurisdiction and import category. It illustrates why steering position alone cannot settle the lighting question, but should not be generalized to every Australian import route, commercial transaction or other destination.

## Put the unanswered question into the purchase terms

A useful decision record can be short. Identify the vehicle and fitted lamps, state the intended destination and import category, describe the proposed operating configuration, and name the evidence still needed. Attach the manufacturer's applicable instructions and any relevant workshop findings. These are recommended commercial safeguards rather than newly asserted legal requirements.

Then separate responsibilities. Who will obtain the destination opinion? Who would perform any proposed work? Who would provide evidence after it? If a change is being proposed, ask for a written scope and a transaction-specific price rather than relying on an assumed conversion cost. This guide makes no claim that suitable parts, software or a permissible conversion necessarily exist for the car.

A sensible reason to postpone payment is an unresolved mismatch between the seller's promise and the destination specialist's answer. For example, the seller may offer temporary adaptation while the specialist is asking for evidence of an acceptable permanent configuration. More screenshots of the same menu do not resolve that disagreement. The missing answer concerns applicability, not the quantity of images supplied.

Proceed only when the evidence supports your particular purchase decision. If the answer remains uncertain, reconsider the offered configuration or vehicle rather than treating adjustment as a guaranteed remedy. No physical inspection or compatibility test of an individual car is represented here.

## Questions buyers ask

### Does a steering-wheel photograph prove lamp compatibility?

No. It does not establish the fitted assemblies, the operating pattern or destination acceptance. Evaluate those questions separately.

### Are temporary masks always unacceptable?

No blanket conclusion is justified. Northern Ireland's testing guidance allows them subject to stated conditions. That is not a universal permanent-import approval.

### Can a workshop confirm everything?

A workshop can address the technical work within its competence. Regulatory acceptance still needs an answer appropriate to the destination and import route; a beam adjustment alone does not provide it.

### Is a travel-mode screenshot useless?

It can help identify a function, but should be connected to the actual vehicle and applicable instructions. It is a starting point, not a complete acceptance record.
