A vehicle order is only as safe as the receivable behind it. When a Chinese exporter agrees to ship on open account, against a usance letter of credit, or with payment after arrival, the gap between handing over a high-value asset and receiving cleared funds is carried by the exporter unless something transfers that risk. China's policy export credit insurer, the China Export & Credit Insurance Corporation (SINOSURE), is the domestic institution built to carry part of that gap. This page explains what its short-term product actually covers, where the limits sit, and how a vehicle exporter applies — without quoting rates or indemnity percentages, which are set case by case and change over time.

## Where short-term export credit insurance sits in the transaction

Short-term export credit insurance protects accounts receivable arising from goods and services exported from China under both documentary-credit and non-documentary-credit payment routes, generally within a short credit horizon. It is not a performance bond, not freight insurance, and not a substitute for buyer due diligence. In a vehicle deal it typically attaches after the exporter holds an approved buyer credit limit and ships conforming goods under an eligible contract.

The practical sequence is: qualify the overseas buyer, apply for a credit limit on that buyer, ship within the approved limit and payment terms, and — if the buyer fails to pay for a covered reason — file a claim. The policy responds to defined causes of non-payment; it does not pay out merely because a deal became unprofitable or because the goods were disputed for genuine non-conformity.

## Two risk families: commercial risk and political risk

SINOSURE short-term cover separates the causes of loss into two families, and a vehicle exporter should read both before choosing payment terms.

- **Commercial risk** originates with the buyer (or the issuing bank under an L/C): insolvency, bankruptcy, refusal to pay without a contractual basis, unilateral termination, or — under a usance credit — an issuing bank that dishonours despite conforming documents.
- **Political risk** originates outside the buyer's control: the buyer's country restricts or bans outward remittance in the contract currency, bans the import or revokes the import licence, imposes a moratorium on payment, or war/civil disturbance prevents performance. A third-country payment route that closes can also fall here.

That distinction matters for vehicle exporters because an L/C removes some buyer commercial risk but not the political risk of a blocked transfer, and open-account terms leave both families with the exporter unless insured. No policy wording should be assumed — the covered events listed in the current SINOSURE product text control.

## Who underwrites it and how a vehicle exporter applies

SINOSURE is China's official export credit insurer; the Ministry of Commerce's short-term export credit insurance handbook frames it as receivables protection for Chinese exporters, while independent international analysis (OECD) notes that short-term business makes up the large majority of SINOSURE's annual cover. There are two practical routes to cover:

1. **Direct policy** with SINOSURE or its local branch for exporters with a regular book of overseas buyers — suitable for a vehicle trader with repeat destination markets.
2. **Indirect / SME channel** through a bank or an integrated-trade (single-window / platform) arrangement for smaller shippers that do not hold their own policy, where cover is arranged alongside financing or settlement.

For both routes the exporter submits buyer information for a credit-limit decision. The limit — not the contract value — is what the insurer has agreed to underwrite, so shipping beyond an approved limit leaves the excess uninsured.

## What a vehicle exporter must verify before relying on cover

| Check | Why it matters for vehicles |
|---|---|
| Approved buyer & limit in writing | Cover follows the named overseas buyer and its approved limit; a new related entity or a higher invoice value is not automatically covered. |
| Payment terms match the policy | Open account, D/P, D/A and usance L/C are treated differently; confirm the chosen instrument and credit period are eligible. |
| Destination country currently insurable | Country risk classification is revised periodically; a market that was coverable last quarter may carry changed conditions now — check the current SINOSURE position before committing. |
| Conforming shipment & documents | Claims require evidence of a valid contract, conforming shipment and overdue collection; VIN-level shipping records and clean documents protect the claim file. |
| Premium & indemnity confirmed in writing | Rates, the indemnity ratio and any maximum are assessed per buyer/country/term — obtain them from SINOSURE for the actual case; do not copy a figure from another deal. |

## What this insurance does not fix

Export credit insurance covers defined non-payment; it does not cure an HS-code problem, a destination that cannot legally register the vehicle, a sanction on the buyer or bank, a quality dispute, or an unlicensed export. Treat it as one gate alongside payment-method design, compliance and proper documentation. If a loss looks like a buyer commercial failure, collect promptly and follow the insurer's overdue-reporting timetable; late reporting can weaken a valid claim.

## Before you book the order

- Confirm the buyer identity and obtain a SINOSURE credit-limit approval before agreeing uninsured open-account terms.
- Match the insured payment route to what the sales contract and proforma actually say.
- Re-confirm the destination country's current risk position and the premium/indemnity terms in writing — all are time-sensitive.
- Keep VIN, B/L, inspection and collection evidence organised so a future claim is supportable.
- Request a Current Export Quotation separately; insurance never sets the vehicle price.

## FAQ
**Does SINOSURE insurance guarantee I get paid?**

No. It indemnifies defined commercial and political non-payment up to an approved limit and the policy indemnity ratio, subject to its terms and claims procedure.

**What is the difference between commercial and political risk here?**

Commercial risk comes from the buyer or issuing bank (insolvency, refusal to pay); political risk comes from state actions such as transfer restrictions, import bans or moratoria.

**Can a small or first-time exporter get cover?**

Yes, typically through a direct policy or an SME/bank or single-window channel; the buyer still needs an approved credit limit.

**Does the policy cover an L/C transaction?**

Short-term cover can extend to documentary-credit routes, including issuing-bank risk under a usance credit; eligibility depends on the current product wording.

**Are premium rates and payout percentages fixed?**

No — they are assessed per buyer, country and term and change over time; obtain the current figures from SINOSURE in writing.
