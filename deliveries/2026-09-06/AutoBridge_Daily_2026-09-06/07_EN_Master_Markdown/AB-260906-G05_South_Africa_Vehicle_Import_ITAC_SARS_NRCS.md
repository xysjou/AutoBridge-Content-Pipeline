South Africa runs vehicle imports through three distinct authorities, and confusing them is the most common reason a shipment stalls. The **International Trade Administration Commission (ITAC)** controls whether the vehicle may be imported at all; the **National Regulator for Compulsory Specifications (NRCS)** controls whether it meets compulsory safety specifications through a Letter of Authority; and the **South African Revenue Service (SARS)** assesses and collects customs duties and tax at clearance. This is a South-Africa-specific guide — it is not a regional rule for neighbouring countries.

## Step order: permit and LOA before the vehicle ships

The South African government's own import guidance is explicit on sequence: before importing a second-hand or used vehicle, goods vehicle or trailer, an importer must first obtain a Letter of Authority from the NRCS, and an import permit is obtained from ITAC (which does not charge a service fee for the permit itself). SARS then collects customs duty calculated under the Customs and Excise schedules. The practical implication for a Chinese exporter is that the buyer should hold the ITAC permit and have the LOA process underway **before** the unit is booked, because arriving without them blocks release.

## ITAC: the import-control gate

- ITAC administers import control under trade law and issues the import permit for controlled goods, including vehicles.
- Used/second-hand vehicle imports are restricted to protect local industry; eligibility depends on the importer category and the vehicle — this is why not every buyer can freely import a used car.
- Permits are delivered to South African addresses and tie to the importer of record; an exporter cannot substitute for the local permit holder.

## NRCS Letter of Authority: the compulsory-specification gate

Every new and used motor vehicle, goods vehicle or trailer imported into South Africa requires an LOA from the NRCS confirming it meets the applicable compulsory specification (the VC-series standards). The NRCS LOA1 application itself references the ITAC permit, showing how the two interlock. A vehicle built for another market — China, Europe, Japan — is not assumed to meet South African specifications; the importer demonstrates compliance, and modification, where feasible, adds cost and time. Whether a specific model is exempt or which exact VC standard applies must be confirmed against the NRCS's current compulsory specification, not assumed.

## SARS: valuation, duty and the border

SARS handles the customs declaration, valuation and collection of duty and tax. The South African government notes customs duty is set as a percentage of customs value under the schedules to the Customs and Excise Act, with separate components applying to a motor-vehicle import.

> This guide states no fixed duty, VAT or ad-valorem percentage. Those figures sit in SARS's current tariff schedules and change; the importer must obtain the current line for the exact HS code and vehicle type. Reported political proposals about higher duties on certain origins are not current law until enacted and must not be treated as the applicable rate.

## Agency map for the exporter to brief the buyer

| Authority | Role in a vehicle import | Document it issues/controls |
|---|---|---|
| ITAC | Import control; used-vehicle restrictions | Import permit |
| NRCS | Compulsory safety/specification compliance | Letter of Authority (LOA) |
| SARS | Customs declaration, valuation, duty & tax collection | Customs clearance / payment receipt |
| Registering authority | Licensing/registration after customs release | Road registration (post-clearance) |

## What the Chinese exporter should verify before payment milestones

1. Confirm the buyer is an eligible importer and has (or is processing) the ITAC permit for the unit category — especially for used vehicles.
2. Establish early whether the specific Chinese-market model can obtain an NRCS LOA; request the VC-standard evidence rather than assuming homologation equivalence.
3. Provide accurate VIN, model year, engine/fuel and specification data; LOA and customs classification both depend on it.
4. Leave duty/VAT computation to SARS's current schedule via the importer's clearing agent; do not quote a tax number in the proforma.
5. Sequence the deposit/balance so final payment is not contingent on the vehicle arriving before permits are ready.

South Africa is a left-hand-traffic market that registers right-hand-drive vehicles; a Chinese left-hand-drive unit faces an additional, often decisive, barrier that should be settled with the buyer and the authorities before production or shipment rather than disputed afterward.

## FAQ
**Do I need both an ITAC permit and an NRCS LOA?**

Yes — they are separate gates: ITAC controls import permission and NRCS the compulsory-safety Letter of Authority; SARS then clears and taxes the goods.

**Who applies for these documents?**

The South African importer of record; the Chinese exporter supplies accurate vehicle data but cannot replace the local permit/LOA holder.

**What is the import duty rate?**

It is set in the current SARS Customs and Excise schedules by HS code and vehicle type — obtain the current figure; this guide gives no fixed percentage.

**Can I import a used Chinese vehicle freely?**

No — used-vehicle imports are restricted and depend on importer eligibility and ITAC approval, plus an NRCS LOA.

**Does a Chinese-market car automatically meet SA specs?**

No; compliance with the NRCS compulsory specification must be demonstrated for the specific model, and steering side is a further consideration.
