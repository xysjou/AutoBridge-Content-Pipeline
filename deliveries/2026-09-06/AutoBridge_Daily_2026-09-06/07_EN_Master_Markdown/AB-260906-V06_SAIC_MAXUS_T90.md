The Interstellar T90 (Xingji T90, formerly the MAXUS T90 in China) is SAIC MAXUS's mid-size, **body-on-frame** double-cab pickup — the type of vehicle bought overseas for load-carrying, rough-road and fleet use rather than passenger refinement. A pickup purchase is decided on bed, payload, drivetrain and chassis, so those lead this page. It covers the **Chinese-market diesel** only; the separate electric T90 is excluded.

## Diesel powertrain and drivetrain

- **Engine**: SAIC π 2.0-litre twin-turbo diesel, 160 kW / 500 N·m, matched to a ZF 8-speed automatic (cross-checked).
- **Drivetrain options**: two-wheel drive, part-time four-wheel drive, and intelligent on-demand four-wheel drive — specify which, since transfer-case and differential content differ.
- **Towing**: a ≈3.5 t maximum braked-towing figure appears in media and on MAXUS global material, but the Chinese-market value is single-source and the global figure is an export-market spec — confirm the number for the actual market build rather than treating them as one.

## Domestic T90 versus export MAXUS T90 — identity caveat

The Chinese name changed from T90 to the 'Interstellar' series, while MAXUS sells an export-market T90 (and regionally named variants such as Tornado). The global MAXUS page lists its own diesel output and a 3.5-tonne braked-towing / 1-tonne-payload specification for the **export** build. Under model-identity discipline, same-nameplate does not prove same-model point-by-point: Chinese and export powertrain, payload, towing and steering are kept market-separate until an OEM document equates them.

## What pickup buyers must verify

1. Choose standard or long bed and obtain OEM/CoC dimensions and rated payload (payload/GVW need the homologation sheet, not a brochure).
2. Select 2WD / part-time 4WD / on-demand 4WD and confirm transfer case, gearing and any differential locks for the terrain.
3. Confirm the diesel emission stage against destination rules and fuel quality (sulphur tolerance matters in some markets).
4. Resolve steering: Chinese market is LHD; RHD export builds (where they exist) are documented separately.
5. Separate the EV T90 entirely — battery, motor and shipping classification differ and are not on this page.
6. Match VIN and bed/drivetrain code across contract, CoC and shipping docs.

## Work-truck export considerations

For fleet and work use, ask about suspension/leaf-spring rating, ground clearance, wading, aftermarket accessory fitment (bull bars, liners) and parts availability. A pickup's value is in its working envelope; secure rated payload, GVW and towing from the homologation/OEM record rather than marketing copy, and request a current export quotation rather than converting the Chinese price.

## FAQ
**Is the Interstellar T90 the same as the export MAXUS T90?**

They share a nameplate, but Chinese and export builds differ in output, payload, towing and steering; they are treated as related-but-market-separate until OEM documents equate them.

**What engine does the Chinese diesel T90 use?**

A SAIC π 2.0T twin-turbo diesel at 160 kW/500 N·m with a ZF 8AT (cross-checked).

**What drivetrains are offered?**

Two-wheel drive, part-time 4WD and on-demand intelligent 4WD, by trim.

**How long is the cargo bed?**

Roughly 1485 mm (standard) or 1800 mm (long-wheelbase); single-source figures to confirm on the OEM sheet.

**Does this page cover the electric T90?**

No — only the Chinese-market diesel; the EV is a separate product with different specs and shipping rules.
