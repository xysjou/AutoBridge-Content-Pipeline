# Refrigerated truck pre-shipment acceptance: build an evidence pack

A refrigerated truck can look complete while the documents still leave important questions unanswered. Does the refrigeration-unit serial number match the quotation? Which operating mode produced the temperature record? Was a reported defect corrected on the vehicle being shipped? An acceptance file should make those answers traceable before dispatch.

This guide recommends a buyer-defined documentary acceptance process, not a universal technical standard or legal approval. Agree its contents with the supplier and use qualified technicians for technical tests under the supplied equipment’s safety instructions. Destination compliance, transport acceptance and commercial release remain separate matters to verify for the actual transaction.

## Start with three identities, not one model name

Chinese specialist publication Truck Home describes the refrigerated vehicle as a combination of chassis, insulated body and refrigeration unit. That is useful background for organizing a purchase file, but its historical article is not an acceptance specification. [Truck Home, 2019](https://www.360che.com/tech/190702/113437.html)

Ask the supplier to identify each part independently. Record the chassis model and VIN, body maker and identification, and refrigeration model and serial number. Link photographs of the identification plates to the offer and the completed vehicle. If a plate or identifier is unavailable, ask for an explanation and alternative traceable documentation rather than filling the gap with a catalogue image.

Create a concise schedule that separates the ordered specification from evidence of what was supplied. The following is a recommended document structure, not a list of statutory requirements.

| Item | Agreed specification record | Evidence to request |
|---|---|---|
| Chassis | Model and VIN | Identification photographs and vehicle documents |
| Insulated body | Maker, identification and agreed dimensions | Drawing revision, measurements and condition photographs |
| Refrigeration unit | Model, serial number and agreed operating modes | Identification plate, applicable manual and installation records |
| Intended application | Cargo and operating conditions | Buyer and supplier agreement |
| Acceptance test | Conditions and pass/fail criteria | Raw logs, instrument records and signed result |
| Outstanding defects | Issue, responsible party and resolution | Correction, retest and written release decision |

Ask separately for the actual completed-vehicle mass and loading documentation. Do not calculate a usable payload from an attractive chassis brochure or an unrelated conversion example.

## Tie drawings to the installed equipment

A generic manual is useful background, but it does not identify the installation delivered. Request the applicable drawings, revision references and a supplier explanation of how they correspond to the truck’s body and unit.

Carrier’s US installation library includes model-specific refrigeration-unit drawings, including Supra body-dimension and mounting documentation. This is a named manufacturer example of the material buyers can ask to see, not proof that another make follows the same design. [Carrier installation instructions](https://www.carrier.com/us/en/cold-chain/truck-trailer/installation-instructions/)

Similarly, FUSO’s European body-options page describes technical drawings, wiring diagrams, specifications and adaptation guidance for Canter and eCanter bodybuilders. Its relevance here is the link between chassis information and body installation. It does not establish approval or warranty coverage for an arbitrary refrigerated conversion. [FUSO body options](https://www.fuso-trucks.com/body-options/)

For the acceptance file, ask the supplier to identify any difference between the agreed drawing and the completed truck. Give each difference a written disposition: accepted change, correction required, or clarification pending. Keep revised drawings alongside the original agreement so that a later reader can understand what changed.

## Agree the temperature demonstration before it begins

A temperature display photograph records a reading, not the complete test conditions. Buyers should agree the demonstration protocol and pass/fail criteria before testing, rather than choosing a favourable interpretation after receiving the result.

The proposed record should identify ambient conditions, setpoint, sensor positions, calibration evidence, loaded or empty state, operating mode, start and finish times, and door-opening events. Ask for the underlying temperature log as well as any summary. Have the parties state what the demonstration is intended to establish and what remains outside its scope.

Carrier’s US Supra S10 specification distinguishes diesel operation from electric standby and associates cooling ratings with ambient and return-air conditions. These published distinctions apply to that named unit; they illustrate why a rating should not be detached from its conditions or treated as the result for a completed truck. [Carrier Supra S10 technical specifications](https://www.carrier.com/us/en/cold-chain/truck-trailer/truck/supra-s10/technical-specifications/)

This guide supplies no universal pull-down time, acceptance temperature or permissible deviation. Those criteria need to be agreed for the actual equipment and intended application. Where several operating modes are included in the purchase, ask which were demonstrated and which still require evidence. Do not silently treat a result from one mode as covering another.

## Document the body and airflow condition

Thermo King Europe recommends matching refrigeration equipment to its application, preserving airflow and maintaining body and door seals. These are manufacturer operating recommendations, not import requirements. [Thermo King application guidance](https://europe.thermoking.com/fuel-economy-cutting-the-cost-of-running-refrigeration-equipment-2)

A more specific example appears in the Thermo King V-520 Series operator’s manual, Revision B, March 2026. Its pretrip checklist covers clean condenser and evaporator airflow paths, functioning drains, secure doors and seals, and cargo-box damage. The checklist does not replace regular maintenance. Its scope is the V-520 Series, not every refrigeration unit. [Thermo King V-520 manual, page 33](https://www.thermoking.com/content/dam/thermoking/documents/products/tk-55032-18-op-en-rev-b.pdf)

For purchasing purposes, ask the technician to document the applicable checks, observations and unresolved issues for the supplied model. Pair photographs with locations and written findings. A photograph of a door alone should not be presented as evidence that every seal or drain has been checked.

## Make the release decision readable

Keep discrepancies, corrective actions and retest evidence with the original results. Identify the vehicle on each record and nominate who is responsible for answering open questions. A replacement unit or changed installation should be reflected in the identifiers and supporting documents.

As a purchasing recommendation, hold release when a material identity mismatch, missing agreed test or unresolved defect prevents the buyer from establishing conformity with the purchase agreement. Record the reason and the evidence needed to resolve it. Any release decision should be explicit and written; a supplier’s statement that the truck is ready does not itself explain what the buyer accepted.

Retain the final specification schedule, photographs, drawings, manuals, raw logs and agreed defect disposition together. This makes the decision understandable without implying that the file guarantees customs clearance, registration or carrier acceptance. ## Frequently asked questions

### Is this acceptance pack a certificate?

No. It is a recommended way to organize buyer and supplier evidence. Any certificate or destination approval needs separate verification of its issuer, scope and applicability to the actual vehicle.

### Can a cold display reading replace a temperature log?

Not for the documentary approach recommended here. Retain the reading’s conditions, timing and sensor information alongside the underlying log and agreed criteria.

### Do the named manufacturer examples apply to other trucks?

No. Carrier, FUSO and Thermo King examples remain limited to the products and regional material identified. Request the supplied equipment’s applicable documents.

### What if a defect is repaired just before shipment?

Ask for the correction record and any agreed retest evidence, then record the buyer’s decision. Keep the original discrepancy visible rather than replacing it with an unexplained final photograph.
