# Reconcile Odometer and Service History Before Buying a Used Car

A low dashboard reading is an offer detail, not a complete history. For an overseas buyer, the useful question is whether dated records support the mileage being sold. A photograph can show what the instrument displays today. It cannot establish how far the vehicle travelled before a replacement cluster, an undocumented period or an earlier sale.

This guide recommends a mileage reconciliation method for purchase decisions. It is not a finding that any seller has committed fraud. US and UK information services are examples with defined coverage, not worldwide databases. The objective is to identify what the records establish, what remains unexplained and whether the proposed purchase should wait.

## Build a dated sequence, not a folder of impressive documents

Ask the seller for the current displayed reading, its unit, the vehicle identification number and the date of capture. Request maintenance invoices, inspection records, previous sale documents and any instrument-replacement paperwork. Retain originals alongside translations. A translation should not silently convert units, correct a date or replace an unclear identifier with a guessed one.

Put each record on a separate line. Record both the service date and the date a document was issued when they differ. Distinguish the mileage recorded during an event from a later description of that event. This avoids treating a recently printed service-history extract as evidence that the car was recently serviced.

[NHTSA's odometer guidance](https://www.nhtsa.gov/vehicle-safety/odometer-fraud) recommends comparing the dashboard with title, maintenance and inspection readings. It also cautions that digital alteration can be difficult to detect. The procurement implication is limited but important: use several kinds of evidence rather than judging credibility from a modern-looking display.

| Evidence | Record exactly | Question it can help answer | What it does not establish alone |
|---|---|---|---|
| Current display | Date, unit, vehicle identity | What is displayed now? | Lifetime distance |
| Service invoice | Event date, reading, workshop, work | Was this reading recorded at a service? | Whether all services are documented |
| Inspection record | Test date, reading, identifier | Does an official event fit the sequence? | Complete maintenance history |
| Instrument replacement record | Old/new readings, date, part and installer | Why did the display change? | Correctness without corroboration |
| History report | Provider, report date, covered markets | Are reported events consistent? | Events outside the provider's data |

These are recommended purchasing records, not a universal legal document list. Request only relevant vehicle information and avoid unnecessary personal details about former owners.

## Separate discrepancies from conclusions

Start by checking whether two apparently conflicting records refer to the same vehicle and the same unit. Preserve the original reading even if you add a converted comparison column. Mark unclear units as unresolved. Do not choose the interpretation that makes the price look attractive.

A later reading below an earlier one deserves explanation, but the discrepancy itself does not identify its cause. Ask whether there was a recording mistake or an instrument replacement and request supporting records. A verbal explanation should become a documented question, not an automatic correction to your file.

For vehicles with a relevant UK history, [GOV.UK's MOT service](https://www.gov.uk/check-mot-history) can show mileage recorded at tests within its coverage. Separately, the [MOT mistake-reporting service](https://www.gov.uk/report-a-mistake-on-your-mot-record) provides a route for corrections. Its mileage-evidence rules require contemporary documents and do not accept a dashboard photograph as proof of the historic reading. Northern Ireland uses a separate route. This is a bounded example of why a seller's explanation and an amended official record are different things.

Do not promise the seller that an error can certainly be corrected. Ask for the outcome from the relevant record holder, retain the original discrepancy and assess the replacement evidence when available.

## Understand what a history report actually covers

For US-history vehicles, the [Department of Justice's NMVTIS consumer page](https://vehiclehistory.bja.ojp.gov/nmvtis_consumers) describes title and brand information and the latest reported odometer reading. That can provide another dated observation. It does not turn one recorded reading into a continuous travel history.

The [NMVTIS FAQ](https://vehiclehistory.bja.ojp.gov/faq/list) explicitly distinguishes its information from repair history. Therefore, a report and a workshop invoice answer different questions. Neither should be renamed a complete service record merely because the other is missing.

If the vehicle moved between countries, map the documents to each period. Ask which provider actually covers each prior registration market. A report returning little information for a foreign vehicle may be demonstrating limited coverage rather than a clean history. Keep that period labelled unknown unless another credible record fills it.

## Give an inspector a mileage question to investigate

A generic inspection request can miss the commercial issue. Tell the independent inspector which dates or readings conflict and ask for a written assessment of relevant evidence. Ask them to identify the inspection's limits, including records or components they could not access. Do not ask them to certify lifetime mileage from appearance alone.

The [FTC's used-car advice](https://consumer.ftc.gov/features/feature-0040-used-cars) distinguishes history reports from independent inspection. Use that distinction in the purchase brief: documents explain reported history, while inspection addresses the vehicle presented. Neither removes the need to resolve a contradictory seller representation.

For a fleet purchase, keep a separate record for each VIN. A clean sample vehicle cannot settle another vehicle's mileage dispute. If the seller supplies one consolidated spreadsheet, request the underlying evidence for disputed rows rather than accepting a batch-level assurance.

## Decide what you are actually willing to buy

Use three purchasing outcomes. First, a sufficiently coherent record may justify continuing negotiations, subject to the remaining condition checks. Second, an explainable but unresolved discrepancy may justify a hold while the issuer supplies evidence. Third, an unsupported mileage claim may make the vehicle unsuitable for a purchase specification that depends on verified distance.

These are editorial decision categories, not certifications. Do not convert “no contradiction found” into “guaranteed genuine mileage.” If you choose to consider a vehicle with unknown distance, ensure the offer describes that uncertainty clearly rather than preserving a precise mileage claim that the evidence cannot support.

Write the requested resolution and the responsible party next to each open question. Before accepting a replacement vehicle in the same order, repeat the reconciliation for its own records. Substitution should not inherit another VIN's conclusion.

## FAQ

### Does a low reading prove light use?

No. It establishes only the current display unless supporting history explains it. Mileage also does not replace a condition assessment or establish how the vehicle was used.

### Is every downward reading fraud?

A contradiction warrants investigation. Do not accuse the seller or declare the mileage genuine without resolving the documents, units and any claimed replacement or recording error.

### Can one history report cover an export vehicle's whole life?

Do not assume that. Confirm countries, periods and data types covered. Request additional records for gaps and distinguish absent information from a positive finding.

### What should stop the purchase?

For a mileage-dependent order, a material unresolved contradiction, refusal to provide relevant evidence or a seller insisting on an unsupported exact claim is a reason to pause or choose another vehicle.
