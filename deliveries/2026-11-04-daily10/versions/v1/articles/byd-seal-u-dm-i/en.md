# BYD SEAL U DM-i MY2026: A Polish-Specification Buying Guide

The BYD SEAL U DM-i presents a useful purchasing dilemma: the version with the largest battery is not the all-wheel-drive version. For an overseas buyer comparing quotations, this means that “higher specification” is too vague to guide the purchase. The right trim depends on the job, the charging routine and the particular car being offered.

This comparison uses Poland's MY2026 specification, published in BYD's January 2026 document. The manufacturer explicitly identifies the model year. Boost, Comfort and Design in that document form the reference set below. These figures are not presented as a global specification or as proof of equipment on a differently sourced car.

## Start with the battery-and-drive combination

[BYD Poland's MY2026 specification](https://www.byd.com/material/byd-site/pl/pdfs/seal-u-dm-i/16012026Seal%20U%20Dmi-0126-BPS-PL-V1_Web_MY2026.pdf) gives the following comparison. All three are plug-in hybrids, combining petrol and electric propulsion.

Comfort is the logical version to investigate when the priority is the largest documented battery and longest certified electric range in this line-up. Design deserves attention when all-wheel drive is a central requirement. Boost provides a separate reference for a buyer who does not need the larger battery or all-wheel drive.

That is a shortlist method, not a claim that one trim will be cheapest to operate. Obtain comparable quotations with the same condition, inspection scope and delivery assumptions. Otherwise, a low asking price may reflect an older vehicle, a different market or missing support rather than better value.

## The important question is where it will charge

A plug-in hybrid should be evaluated together with a realistic charging routine. The [Energy Saving Trust's consumer guidance](https://energysavingtrust.org.uk/advice/electric-vehicles/) emphasises regular charging when discussing PHEV efficiency. This is general advice, not a measurement of this BYD or a promise about a buyer's fuel bill.

For a private buyer, identify the parking place used most nights and whether charging there is practical. For a fleet, establish who is responsible for connecting the car, whether drivers return to a depot, and how a missed charging opportunity would affect the next shift. A car that fits an idealised commute may be less suitable for a working pattern that changes every day.

Do not use the published electric range as a rigid distance allowance. Consider climate, traffic, passengers and the route, and ask for a representative demonstration where feasible. Preserve the distinction between electric-only distance and a journey using both energy sources. A seller describing total travel distance without explaining the contribution of petrol is not answering the electric-use question.

A useful buyer enquiry asks for recent evidence of normal charging, not just a dashboard showing a full battery. Request a demonstration on suitable equipment, confirm the cables and accessories included, and discuss any warning messages with a qualified service provider. The purpose is to establish that the supplied vehicle fits the intended routine.

## Charger size and charging speed are different things

The [Energy Saving Trust's charging explanation](https://energysavingtrust.org.uk/advice/charging-electric-vehicles/) notes that a chargepoint can offer more power than a vehicle can receive. This general principle matters when comparing the BYD with a battery-electric alternative. Access to a powerful public charger does not automatically mean that a PHEV will use that charger's full output.

Ask which charging opportunity provides useful time in the normal day. Overnight or workplace access may be more relevant than a rapid-charge stop made solely to recover electric range. Review the actual tariffs and parking conditions separately; no charging-cost estimate is assumed here.

If charging will be installed at a business or home, obtain advice from an appropriately qualified local installer. Check the vehicle specification against the proposed installation and avoid promising a fixed charging time before the equipment and conditions are established. A commercial quotation should identify whether any charging equipment or installation is included.

## Inspect both sides of the powertrain

The enquiry should not stop at the battery. Ask for the maintenance record covering the petrol engine as well as the electric system, and commission an inspection appropriate to a plug-in hybrid. Request an explanation of warning history and repairs rather than accepting that quiet operation proves a complete bill of health.

Agree a normal driving demonstration that lets the inspector assess the car without unsafe manoeuvres. Ask them to explain what was checked, what could not be tested, and what remains dependent on specialist diagnosis. Keep the report associated with the vehicle identity and mileage at inspection.

For family use, inspect the cabin with the intended occupants and luggage rather than relying entirely on dimensions. Try the seating position, access to controls and installation of the actual child restraint if relevant. For a business fleet, examine how drivers will learn the operating modes and report faults. Practical details may resolve a shortlist more decisively than a small difference in quoted equipment.

## A safety badge still needs the correct variant

The [Euro NCAP SEAL-U assessment](https://www.euroncap.com/assessments/byd/seal-u/1046/) identifies Electric Design as the original tested variant and separately lists PHEV variants in its applicability table. Read that table rather than assume every similarly named vehicle was the crash-test car. The older family assessment alone is not treated here as independent confirmation of every Polish MY2026 vehicle.

Ask the supplying dealer or manufacturer to confirm the relevant applicability when that assessment is important to the purchase. Separately, investigate the actual vehicle's accident and repair history. A model assessment cannot certify that a particular used car retains its original restraints, sensors or body condition.

The same discipline applies to software and assistance features. Have important functions demonstrated on the supplied car, in the required language and with the intended account arrangement. Equipment descriptions do not establish that a connected feature will remain available at the destination.

## Turn the shortlist into a clear enquiry

Identify the Polish MY2026 trim, required drive configuration, actual vehicle identity, condition and included accessories. Ask for a dated equipment document and written clarification of discrepancies. If the seller proposes another market's car, restart the comparison using that market's documents rather than carrying these figures over.

Confirm who can support the car after delivery. Where warranty coverage is offered, ask for terms applicable to the VIN and destination. Keep vehicle price, transport, inspection and destination work separate enough that offers can be compared fairly. Registration eligibility and legal requirements need destination-specific checking; this model comparison does not establish them.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is Design the longest-range electric option?

Not in the Polish MY2026 specification used here. Comfort has the highest published combined electric WLTP range. Design is the all-wheel-drive option.

### Can I add the engine and motor outputs to calculate total power?

Use the manufacturer's stated total system power rather than adding component maxima. Component ratings do not replace the published system figure.

### Does a larger battery guarantee lower fuel costs?

No. Assess charging access, actual use and local energy prices. This article provides no guaranteed running-cost saving.

### Are these figures valid for a China-market vehicle with a related name?

No. A different model name, market or specification needs its own evidence; no alias or equivalence is assumed.
