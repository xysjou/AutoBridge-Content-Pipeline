# XPENG G6: Choosing the 2024 German Launch Version

The strongest reason to compare an early XPENG G6 carefully is that its trim names describe more than equipment. In the German range introduced in September 2024, choosing Standard Range, Long Range or Performance changes the battery or driven wheels. A seller can therefore offer two cars that look broadly similar while leaving the buyer with substantially different ownership priorities.

For buyers assessing used stock or an export quotation, the sensible starting point is the car's original specification, not today's model headline. This article examines the September 2024 German launch specification. That date identifies a documented edition, not a manufacturer-certified model year for every vehicle. A registration date alone does not establish the edition. Later cars, other markets and undocumented imports need their own specification evidence.

## Three versions, three buying priorities

The figures below come from [XPENG's German launch announcement](https://www.xpeng.com/de/news/0191aa07fd9f918dc1e58a02828e0023). They describe battery-electric vehicles supplied under that reference specification.

| Parameter | RWD Standard Range | RWD Long Range | AWD Performance |
|---|---|---|---|
| Market and edition | Germany, September 2024 launch | Germany, September 2024 launch | Germany, September 2024 launch |
| Powertrain | BEV | BEV | BEV |
| Driven wheels | Rear | Rear | All |
| Net battery capacity | 66 kWh | 87.5 kWh | 87.5 kWh |
| Battery chemistry | LFP | NCM | NCM |
| Maximum motor output | 190 kW | 210 kW | 350 kW |
| Combined range | 435 km WLTP | 570 km WLTP | 550 km WLTP |

The table suggests a useful purchasing order: establish whether the buyer needs a larger battery, then decide whether all-wheel drive is worth prioritising. It does not establish which car is best for every destination. A fleet with predictable daily routes may place more value on an acceptable acquisition quotation and dependable overnight charging than on the largest published range figure. A buyer with variable intercity travel may prefer to investigate Long Range first. Someone specifically seeking all-wheel drive should evaluate Performance rather than assume that a larger battery automatically includes it.

These are buying recommendations, not promises about real-world consumption, resale value or operating cost. In particular, the shared nominal capacity of the two larger-battery versions does not make them interchangeable vehicles.

## A charging claim needs a charging context

XPENG's launch publication lists peak DC charging power of 215 kW for Standard Range and up to 280 kW for Long Range. Those are manufacturer claims for the reference versions, not guaranteed average charging power throughout a stop.

A practical quotation should be accompanied by more useful evidence than a photograph of a charging screen showing an impressive number. Ask for the exact trim, charging connector photographs, battery identification and a recent charging demonstration with its starting and finishing state of charge. If the seller supplies a session log, retain the date and conditions with it. A result without this context is difficult to compare with another vehicle.

For a business planning repeated routes, map charging opportunities before deciding that a particular peak is commercially valuable. Consider where the car will actually stop, whether a suitable charger is accessible during working hours, and whether a delayed charging session would disrupt the next booking. Do not turn a manufacturer's best-case charging claim into a fixed delivery-time commitment to a customer.

Battery chemistry also deserves a place in the enquiry. In this launch range, the Standard Range specification differs from the larger-battery versions. Ask for the instructions applicable to the supplied battery rather than accepting a generic charging routine copied from another G6. A seller's description of “the big battery” is not a substitute for an identifiable pack and matching documentation.

## Do not give an earlier car a later specification

XPENG announced revised G6 and G9 models in Germany in [July 2025](https://www.xpeng.com/de/news/0197f5ede48c97e8d9368a02811e004b). That separate announcement is a strong reason to keep the September 2024 specification distinct. A recent webpage or an attractive advertisement may describe a revision that the offered vehicle does not have.

Request a dated equipment sheet linked to the vehicle identification number, the original sales market, and photographs of the actual car. Where the seller has assembled a listing from several documents, ask which document controls the sale. An updated infotainment screen does not, by itself, prove a change in battery hardware or charging capability.

For a multi-car order, review each vehicle rather than treating the first example as representative of the entire lot. A sensible allocation sheet separates trim, original market, build documentation and included accessories. This is particularly important when the seller is offering mixed used stock under one commercial description.

## Read the safety assessment beyond the headline

The [2024 Euro NCAP assessment](https://www.euroncap.com/assessments/xpeng/g6/1086/) reported a stable passenger compartment in the frontal offset test and maximum points in the side barrier and pole impact tests. It also rated the rear passenger's chest protection as weak in the full-width rigid barrier test. These findings belong to the tested Long Range LHD configuration and assessment conditions; they are not a guarantee about an individual used car.

That distinction matters for family and passenger-service buyers. Examine the actual restraint equipment, seat adjustment and intended child-seat installation, and commission an appropriate inspection of any accident-repair history. A published assessment cannot establish that a supplied car retains its original airbags, sensors or structural condition.

Likewise, driver assistance should be demonstrated as assistance. Ask the seller to show relevant settings and warning behaviour in a suitable, supervised demonstration. Do not place an order on the assumption that the car can take responsibility for driving.

## What would justify progressing the quotation?

A G6 quotation becomes easier to judge when it contains a coherent vehicle identity and a usable aftersales plan. Ask who will diagnose the vehicle in the destination, where replacement parts would come from, and which party will respond if software or charging functions do not work as described. Seek written answers for the specific car rather than relying on the existence of a brand website somewhere else.

Separate the vehicle price from transport, inspection, accessories and any destination work in the commercial discussion. No landed-cost estimate is implied here. If the seller cannot reconcile the trim with the battery and drive configuration, postpone a firm commitment until the discrepancy is explained. An attractive price does not remove the cost of buying the wrong version.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is the longest-range launch G6 also the most powerful?

No. In the German September 2024 specification, RWD Long Range has the highest published combined WLTP range, while AWD Performance has the highest motor output. Choose according to the intended job rather than assuming the top performance version leads every measure.

### Can I treat WLTP range as a guaranteed route distance?

No. Use the labelled WLTP figures to understand the documented specification. For an actual route, request appropriate vehicle-specific evidence and retain an operating margin rather than promising a journey based solely on the laboratory figure.

### Does this establish that a China-market G6 has the same equipment?

No. The reference is the German launch range. A China-market vehicle needs its own trim, battery, charging and software documentation. Similar names or bodywork do not establish equivalent specification.

### Is this a current availability or warranty offer?

No. Confirm stock, condition, warranty eligibility and cross-border service arrangements for the actual vehicle before agreeing terms.
