## What PSI Actually Is
Pre-Shipment Inspection is verification **against the L/C, pro forma invoice or contract** of **quality, specification, quantity/weight, packaging and shipping marks**, with some destinations additionally requiring **loading supervision**. For vehicles it doubles as the buyer's last chance to catch a misdescribed, damaged or tampered unit **before** it crosses the rail of a vessel — correction after sailing is slow and expensive.

## Choose the Inspection Body Against the Destination Rule
- Internationally recognised bodies include **CCIC, Bureau Veritas (BV) and SGS**.
- The critical nuance: **some destinations mandate a named body and programme (e.g., PVoC / CoC)** before the goods can clear. A generic report from a different, unmandated inspector will not satisfy that destination even if it is thorough.
- Which countries mandate PSI, and which body they designate, is **country-specific and time-sensitive** — verify it on the destination's official conformity programme before appointing an inspector; this guide does not generalise a country list.

## Vehicle-Specific Inspection Items
A fit-for-purpose vehicle PSI goes beyond a walk-around. Documented vehicle inspection content covers:
- **VIN and engine-number consistency** across chassis stamping, engine bay, documents and the contract;
- **Anti-fraud screen** — assembled/spliced, smuggled or stolen-status indicators;
- **History/condition** — **accident, flood/water and fire** evidence;
- **Chassis/undercarriage**, **brakes**, and **dashboard fault/warning lights** (read and clear-record where possible);
- **Odometer/mileage** plausibility and signs of tampering;
- For **EVs/hybrids**, add battery/SOH screen, charge-state and warning-state checks, and feed results into the ocean dangerous-goods paperwork; under IMDG Amendment 42-24 (mandatory from 2026-01-01) a lithium-ion-battery-powered vehicle is classified UN3556, not the legacy generic UN3171; apply documentation/SOC details per the current EV shipping Fact Sheet and the carrier (see the EV ocean-compliance guide).

## Commissioning Documents and a Usable Report
- A formal **inspection entrustment letter** is typically required, **stamped/confirmed by the importer (buyer)** — the buyer's authorisation is part of what makes the report valid for their purposes.
- The **inspection certificate should list the items inspected, the result for each, and any rectification** — a one-page "passed" statement with no item-level findings is weak evidence in a dispute.
- Tie the report to **VIN(s), date, location and photos**, and sequence it **before container loading** so findings can be acted on (see the loading/lashing guide for the hand-off into stowage).

## Process Standardisation at the Base
Export bases can embed PSI inside a quality system — documented practice describes an **ISO 9001** approach that standardises **selection → inspection/reconditioning → customs clearance → after-sales** (single-source). The buyer value is repeatability: the same item-level checklist on every unit rather than inspector-by-inspector variation.

## What This Guide Recommends You Specify in the Inspection Order
1. **Destination rule first** — mandatory programme and designated body confirmed officially.
2. **Item-level checklist** (VIN/engine match, fraud, accident/flood/fire, chassis, brakes, fault lights, mileage) plus EV battery screen.
3. **Buyer-stamped entrustment** and a report carrying **results and rectification**, not just a verdict.
4. **Photo + VIN + date/location** evidence, timed **before loading**, with optional **loading supervision**.
5. A defined **rectification/re-inspection loop** before the unit is released to stowage.

## Frequently Asked Questions
**Does any recognised inspector's report work everywhere?** No — some destinations mandate a specific body/programme (PVoC/CoC); confirm the designated inspector for the destination.
**What does a vehicle PSI check?** VIN/engine-number consistency, fraud/splicing, accident/flood/fire history, chassis, brakes, dashboard fault lights and mileage, plus battery checks for EVs.
**Who must authorise the inspection?** Typically an entrustment letter stamped by the importer/buyer.
**What makes a report strong evidence?** Item-level findings with results and rectification, linked to VIN, date, location and photos — not a bare "passed".
**When should PSI happen?** Before container loading/RoRo delivery, so defects can still be corrected.
