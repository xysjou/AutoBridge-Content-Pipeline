# Deepal E07 MY25 in Australia: RWD or AWD Performance for your load and route?

The Deepal E07 poses a more useful buying question than whether an electric SUV can look like a pickup: does its reconfigurable rear area solve a real carrying problem, and which drivetrain suits the buyer's priorities? For the Australian reference examined here, RWD has the higher estimated combined WLTP range, while AWD Performance has the higher power and torque figures. Neither choice removes the need to check cargo dimensions, charging access and the documentation for a particular vehicle.

The reference is DEEPAL Australia's MY25 specification sheet, edition v1:2 with data as at September 2026 and document code DPL7855/MY25/09.26. The publication date does not turn it into MY26. Review headlines that use a different year do not replace the document's MY25 identity. This is the Australian launch-generation, five-seat battery-electric E07, with no claim that another market's similarly named vehicle has the same specification.

## Start with the load you actually carry

The E07's rear glass and tailgate arrangement changes access to the cargo area. That makes the shape of a buyer's equipment more relevant than a dramatic photograph of the rear body. Measure the objects you intend to transport, decide how often the rear seats need to remain in use, and ask the seller for a demonstration consistent with the vehicle's official instructions. Do not assume that an opening shown in a photograph is an approved driving configuration.

The Australian sheet lists 131 litres of front storage, 524 litres in the enclosed rear area and 1654 litres with the rear seats folded. These describe storage volume, not a road payload rating. They also do not establish the number of particular cases or boxes that fit. Request opening and floor measurements for your objects and keep weight limits as a separate question. The “Multitruck” description is not evidence that this vehicle can replace a work ute for every load.

RWD is the logical first comparison for a buyer who prioritises the higher rated range. AWD Performance answers a different preference: more quoted power and torque, with all-wheel drive. The common battery capacity does not make the two range figures interchangeable, and the AWD badge alone is not proof of suitability for a particular off-road route. Base the shortlist on your intended use rather than assuming that the more powerful version improves every aspect of ownership.

The WLTP values remain laboratory estimates. They do not promise the same distance with a trailer, a full load or particular weather. This article does not substitute an invented real-world figure. For route planning, the practical question is where you can charge the specific vehicle and what contingency you need when a planned stop is unavailable.

## Charging: distinguish a maximum from a plan

The sheet quotes a maximum DC rate of 240 kW and a claimed 30–80% charging window of 15 minutes. Those are manufacturer figures for the stated window, not a full-charge time, a sustained rate throughout a session or a promise at every charger. Ask about the conditions behind a seller's charging claim and whether the proposed infrastructure suits the offered vehicle.

The listed AC maximum is 7 kW, with Type 2 for AC and CCS2 for DC. However, official descriptions of AC charging duration disagree, so no definitive AC full-charge time is given here. Ask DEEPAL or the responsible supplier for clarification tied to the vehicle and intended installation. Do not plan an overnight schedule by choosing whichever published duration looks more convenient. The maximum power and the elapsed time are different questions.

## Size and towing need separate checks

The Australian sheet gives dimensions of 5045 mm long, 1996 mm wide and 1680 mm high, with a 3120 mm wheelbase. Check your parking area, access and loading space rather than treating SUV classification as a guarantee of an easy fit. A demonstrator can help you assess access, but any judgement about ease of use is yours, not an AutoBridge road-test finding.

For both variants, the sheet lists maximum braked towing of 1500 kg and unbraked towing of 750 kg. These are Australian specification figures, not a destination approval or a calculation for a particular laden combination. A buyer intending to tow should request the vehicle's relevant towing and weight documentation, suitable equipment details and confirmation of the rules applicable where it will operate. Do not infer payload by subtracting unrelated published masses or treat a stationary platform capacity as an on-road carrying limit.

## Ownership details to put on the demonstration list

The sheet describes front double-wishbone and rear multi-link suspension with air suspension. It also lists a 15.4-inch touchscreen and a head-up display. These details suggest useful questions about the controls and maintenance support, not a basis for claiming a particular ride quality. Try the interface functions you expect to use and ask who can support the specific vehicle at its destination.

The stated spare provision is a puncture repair kit rather than a spare wheel. For someone travelling far from convenient assistance, that deserves attention in the ownership plan. Ask what is included with the actual unit and how the intended journey would be supported. No roadside repair procedure or assurance that a kit can solve every tyre problem follows from the equipment listing.

## Questions before choosing a version

### Is this a 2026 model because the sheet is dated September 2026?

No. The reference document explicitly carries MY25 in its code. Confirm the individual vehicle's identity and build information rather than replacing that designation with the publication year.

### Does AWD Performance offer the longer quoted range?

No. The Australian sheet estimates 510 km combined WLTP for AWD Performance and 550 km for RWD. AWD Performance has the higher listed power and torque.

### Can the cargo volume establish payload?

No. Litres describe space. Ask for the exact vehicle's weight limits and loading documentation, especially if the reconfigurable rear area is central to the purchase.

### Is an Australian specification enough for an overseas order?

No. Availability, current price, export eligibility, registration, warranty applicability and local support need separate confirmation. The comparison does not establish China-market equivalence or specifications for another powertrain. Pause a deposit if the seller cannot link the promised drivetrain, charging details and loading documentation to the offered vehicle.

A focused enquiry should state the drivetrain, MY25 reference edition, intended cargo and charging arrangement, then request VIN-linked evidence.
