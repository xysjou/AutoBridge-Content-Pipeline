# Spare-Key Checks Before Taking Delivery of a Vehicle

A seller places several keys on the desk and describes the vehicle as having a complete set. That is a useful beginning, not a finished handover. The buyer still needs to know what each physical item does, whether it is authorized for the particular vehicle, and what happened to any missing keys. For an overseas buyer or a fleet manager, an unresolved answer can remain a purchasing issue even when the vehicle starts during a demonstration.

Think of the handover as two related records: the physical items delivered and the evidence about their functions and authorization. This is a recommended purchasing method, not a legal requirement for a particular number of keys. The manufacturer examples below are limited to their stated models and markets. They do not prove that every imported car has the same key types, menus, replacement support or security arrangements.

## A door-opening demonstration is not the whole answer

Toyota’s US guide for the 2024 Toyota 86 distinguishes a mechanical copy that opens the door from an immobilizer-authorized key that starts the engine. It also recommends keeping a spare and key-code information securely. The distinction explains why the buyer should ask what the seller means by “spare”: an item that opens a lock is not necessarily evidence of starting authorization.

For the offered vehicle, request a separate demonstration of each promised function for each supplied physical key. Ask the seller or an appropriate authorized provider to conduct it according to the applicable owner instructions. Record which item was demonstrated and what was observed. A single successful start should not be used as the record for every object placed beside the vehicle.

Keep the exercise focused on the purchase, not on learning how to defeat or program a security system. This guide does not provide programming, pairing sequences or bypass instructions. If a function cannot be shown clearly, record it as unresolved and ask for an appropriate service check. Do not convert an assumption into a confirmed item simply because the exterior shell looks correct.

## List key types, not just a total

Volvo’s Canadian guidance for the XC40 2022 distinguishes standard keys, buttonless keys and Care Key. Care Key can impose a maximum-speed restriction. This is a concrete reason not to assume that two physical objects provide identical functionality. It is not a claim that every Volvo or another brand uses those same categories.

For each item offered, write down its type, the function promised by the seller and the demonstration result. For a fleet, also identify the vehicle to which the item belongs. Use a private inventory that can distinguish the items without publishing key codes or sensitive identifiers. There is no benefit in putting a readable security code into a public sales photograph or article.

The US Tesla Model 3 guidance provides another example: it lists keys using type icons and says key-fob availability varies by market and configuration. The buying question is therefore whether the supplied physical cards or fobs match the actual vehicle’s configuration, not whether another market’s advertisement shows a different accessory. Do not treat an unavailable key type as a missing entitlement without confirming what applies to this unit.

## A replacement and a missing key are separate questions

Volvo’s Canadian XC40 2022 instructions tell owners replacing a lost key to bring the remaining keys to the workshop and have the lost remote-key code erased. The cited model also displays its registered key count. These facts support asking about both the keys being delivered and the status of those that are not. They do not establish that every vehicle exposes the same count or uses the same service procedure.

Tesla’s US support material likewise describes adding physical key cards or fobs and removing keys as separate actions; a newly paired key appears in the key list. Its US Model 3 guidance advises removing lost key cards after replacement. Receiving a new physical item should therefore not, by itself, be accepted as documentary proof that a missing item has been dealt with.

Ask whether any keys have been lost or replaced and request the relevant service evidence, where available. If the particular vehicle provides a registered-key record, ask the seller or authorized provider to reconcile it with the delivered items. A mismatch calls for clarification; it is not a reason to invent the location of a missing key or to claim that a particular vehicle has been compromised.

## Establish a replacement route before relying on a promise

Ford’s cited US manual section directs replacement-key enquiries to an authorized dealer and asks for the original key-tag number where available. This is a manufacturer-specific example of using the proper replacement channel. It does not promise that a dealer in the destination country can support every imported Ford, and this guide makes no specific fitment claim from that manual section.

Before buying, ask an appropriate authorized provider whether it can support the actual vehicle, its original sales market and the relevant physical key type. Keep identifying information in the necessary private service discussion rather than in a public listing. Request a clear description of what remains to be checked. Replacement prices, availability and lead times are not established by the sources in this guide, so obtain a current vehicle-specific answer rather than budgeting from an unsupported estimate.

Clarify who is expected to resolve any outstanding key issue and what evidence will show that it is resolved. A vague promise that “another key can be arranged later” leaves both the function and the route unconfirmed. If the seller offers a replacement as part of the deal, describe that item and the intended service outcome in the purchase discussion instead of treating the promise as already completed.

## Make the handover record reflect the evidence

| Handover question | Useful evidence | If unresolved |
|---|---|---|
| What physical items are included? | Private item-by-item inventory tied to the vehicle | Ask for a corrected delivery description |
| What does each item do? | Separate demonstration of promised functions | Seek an appropriate service check |
| Were keys lost or replaced? | Seller disclosure and relevant service record | Clarify the missing-key position |
| Does an available registration record match? | Vehicle-specific reconciliation by the seller or provider | Resolve the discrepancy, without guessing |
| Can the actual vehicle be supported? | Current answer from an appropriate authorized provider | Do not rely on an unsupported replacement promise |

A buyer can accept the documented functions, seek a defined remedy for a missing or nonworking item, or defer handover when the unresolved issue is material to the purchase. Those are commercial choices, not a claim that all transactions require a universal two-key rule. Make the agreed delivery description specific enough that “complete set” has a clear meaning for this vehicle.

This article covers physical keys, cards, fobs and immobilizer-related handover evidence. It does not assess connected-account ownership or phone-account transfer. Recheck the applicable manual revision and current local service support before purchase; the examples are not a substitute for vehicle-specific confirmation.

## Frequently asked questions

### Does a metal blade prove that a key can start the vehicle?

No. Toyota’s 2024 US 86 example explicitly distinguishes mechanical entry from immobilizer-authorized starting. Confirm the promised functions on the actual vehicle.

### Is every vehicle required to come with two keys?

This guide establishes no such universal requirement. Agree the number, types and functions included in the particular offer.

### Does adding a replacement prove the lost key was removed?

No. The Volvo and Tesla examples treat the missing-key status as a separate matter. Ask for vehicle-specific evidence of the appropriate service outcome.

### Can a destination dealer always replace an imported vehicle’s key?

That is not guaranteed here. Confirm support for the vehicle and market with an appropriate authorized provider before relying on replacement availability.
