# Leapmotor B10: Comparing Malaysia’s Life and Design Electric SUVs

A B10 quotation becomes useful only when it says which B10 it covers. For a buyer comparing the Malaysian Life and Design versions, the meaningful choice is not simply a better-equipped SUV against a cheaper one. The battery, rated range and maximum DC charging power change together. That combination deserves attention before a buyer starts comparing screen sizes or trim descriptions.

This article uses the Malaysian battery-electric B10 specification announced on 10 December 2025. It is a regional product reference, not a claim that a China-market vehicle, a later production revision or a B10 Hybrid EV has identical equipment. There has been no first-hand drive or physical inspection for this article. Its purpose is to explain what the documented differences mean for a sensible shortlist and what remains specific to the actual vehicle offered.

## The two Malaysian versions in context

Stellantis Malaysia lists a 56.2 kWh battery for Life and 67.1 kWh for Design. Their combined WLTP range figures are 361 km and 434 km respectively. Both are listed with 11 kW AC charging; maximum DC power is 140 kW for Life and 168 kW for Design. The same release gives a 30–80% DC charging interval of under 20 minutes. Those are manufacturer figures for the stated versions, not measured results from an AutoBridge road test. [Malaysia launch specification](https://www.media.stellantis.com/as-en/leapmotor/press/the-all-new-electric-leapmotor-b10-arrives-in-malaysia-to-redefine-accessible-innovation-from-rm107-800)

The practical comparison is therefore a pair of operating profiles. A buyer who can charge reliably between predictable local journeys should ask whether the smaller battery already meets the route with a useful reserve. A buyer facing less predictable journey lengths should investigate the larger battery, without assuming that extra rated range removes the need for charging stops. Neither judgement follows from the trim name alone. The driver's routine, parking access and tolerance for an unscheduled stop are more informative than treating the highest number as an automatic winner.

## Read the range column before judging it

The Malaysian launch table also publishes NEDC figures. This article deliberately uses WLTP figures for the comparison; switching cycles halfway through would make one specification look better without changing the car. A quotation that presents only a distance number leaves an important question unanswered. Ask the supplier to write the cycle alongside the number and to identify the exact battery version behind it.

For a purchase decision, separate a brochure comparison from a route plan. The brochure helps distinguish Life from Design under a named procedure. A route plan starts with the journeys that matter to the buyer: the longest routine trip, likely charging opportunities and a workable alternative if the preferred charger is occupied. Avoid converting the brochure figure into a promised motorway distance. That would require evidence this document does not supply. A buyer can still make a useful preliminary decision without pretending to know an exact real-world result.

## Charging power and charging time answer different questions

The DC figures describe the maximum stated power, while the time claim describes a defined state-of-charge window. A higher peak is not a promise that power remains at that level throughout a session. Equally, the quoted 30–80% interval does not describe a near-empty-to-full charge. Keep the interval visible when comparing the B10 with another SUV, or ask for both vehicles' figures over the same interval before drawing a conclusion.

For an overnight-charging owner, the shared AC specification may be more relevant than the difference in DC peaks. The useful next step is to match the intended parking arrangement with a suitable installation assessment, rather than choosing the car solely around a rapid-charging headline. For an intercity user, examine the charging stops on the actual route. A strong vehicle-side number cannot establish the access arrangements, service condition or availability of a particular charging location.

## The body is an SUV, not a substitute for a seating check

As a separate cross-check on the model's body dimensions, the UK B10 press information lists a length of 4,515 mm, width of 1,885 mm, height of 1,655 mm and wheelbase of 2,735 mm. The UK release describes a single-trim sales approach, so its equipment list should not be copied onto either Malaysian trim. Shared dimensions are not proof of a shared specification package. [UK B10 press information](https://www.media.stellantis.com/uk-en/leapmotor/press/leapmotor-b10-uk-press-information)

A useful showroom assessment would start with the people and objects that normally travel together. Set the front seats for the intended occupants, then assess rear access and the available luggage shape. Leave the brochure's equipment ranking aside during this exercise. A seat that adjusts electrically is not, by itself, evidence that a particular occupant will find the driving position comfortable. Likewise, a cabin photograph does not settle how a child seat, walking aid or bulky case will fit. These are inspection questions, not defects claimed about the B10.

## Safety evidence has a defined vehicle scope

Euro NCAP's 2025 B10 assessment identifies a left-hand-drive Design as the tested model and lists applicability across specified electric variants, including left- and right-hand-drive entries. Its written findings include a stable passenger compartment in the frontal offset test and good protection in the side tests. It also identifies limitations, including the absence of protection for pedestrians behind the car in the assessed reversing scenario. A balanced reading includes those details rather than treating a star result as universal protection. [Euro NCAP B10 assessment](https://www.euroncap.com/assessments/1217/)

For a particular offer, compare the vehicle identity and safety equipment against that applicability record. A test report does not establish the condition of an individual car or replace the operating instructions for its assistance systems. The driver still needs to understand which warnings and interventions are present, how they are controlled and where their limitations are documented.

## Questions worth resolving before a quotation becomes an order

**Is Design always the better purchase?** No universal conclusion follows from the documented battery difference. The additional rated range matters most when it solves an identified usage problem. A buyer with dependable charging and shorter trips may reach a different conclusion from a buyer using variable intercity routes.

**Can UK equipment be assumed on Malaysian Life?** No. The official market releases describe different sales structures. Use the Malaysian trim schedule and the offered vehicle's documentation rather than merging regional lists.

**Does the B10 name establish export suitability?** No. This model reference does not verify a shipment's destination approval, registration, warranty transfer, stock condition or current commercial availability. Resolve those separately for the actual offer.

The clearest B10 shortlist begins with Life and Design on the same WLTP basis, then tests their differences against the intended charging routine. That approach keeps the choice focused on the vehicle the buyer can identify, rather than a composite B10 assembled from attractive numbers found in different markets.
